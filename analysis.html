There is no single "maximum" — the answer depends on the effective date

The current annual percentage changes every March 1, and the allowable amount depends on the effective date. Rules § 4.12(a) states a 12-month-from-availability banking test, while the SF.gov summary says 24 full months without an increase; F-16 remains open. Tables using a banked portion apply the Rule’s 12-month wording only as a working assumption, not a resolved legal interpretation. Every scenario below has an explicit effective date. Figures are computed from 2026-11-13 and are reproducible with python3 scripts/verify_calculations.py.

The 12-month / 24-month question — unresolved (F-16)

Two official Rent Board pages describe banking in different terms. Rules § 4.12(a) says a full 12 months must elapse from when an annual allowance could have been imposed; the SF.gov banked-increases summary says at least 24 full months must pass without any increase. The summary does not make clear in that sentence whether its clock is global or per skipped allowance. The examples favor the Rule’s reading in one scenario, but the sources do not expressly reconcile the wording. F-16 remains open.

Answer to the question as asked: raising both units is not a legal safe harbour and is not required. Discrimination law prohibits different treatment because of a protected characteristic; it does not require identical percentages. The protection comes from a documented, neutral, unit-specific reason — not from equalising numbers. sf.gov — Housing discrimination · CA Civil Rights Department · HUD

Calculation rules that invalidate an otherwise plausible number

  • No rounding up to the nearest dollar. sf.gov — Rent increases
  • No compounding. Banked percentages are added, then applied once to the current base rent. sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Use the rate in effect on the effective date, not the date the notice is served. sf.gov — Rent increases
  • Base rent only. Parking and storage are generally part of base rent; temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges are not. sf.gov — Irregular periods
  • Once every 12 months from the last increase or the move-in date. sf.gov — Irregular periods
  • A current license on the effective date, or the entire increase is null and void. Rules § 13.14
  • A non-conforming notice can render the entire increase null and void. Rules § 4.10(b)
