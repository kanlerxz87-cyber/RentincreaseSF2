Rent Board notice vs. tenant notice

As an individual owner, you are subject to the same Housing Inventory requirement as other residential property owners. Report the unit; a tenant-occupied unit receives a Rent Increase License. The license must be current before the rent increase takes effect. The official guidance describes written notice to the tenant but does not instruct a separate filing of each increase notice with the Rent Board; the license does not need to accompany the tenant notice. Rules § 13.14 states the test in terms: “the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used to determine whether or not the increase conforms.” While the formal deadline is the effective date, verify the license before serving notice as the lower-risk sequence. sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Rent increases

One unit or both?

No official source requires raising both units. The Rent Ordinance applies to each unit based on its own base rent and rent history. Unit 1's current annual allowance is 1.6%; a 3.0% scenario on 2027-02-01 includes a conditional 1.4% banked portion; Unit 2's 13.1% is only a conditional scenario requiring the complete ledger and every other gate, including F-28; a conservative annual-only scenario is 1.6% ($50.40 → $3,200.40), leaving 11.5% banked. Applying 1.6% to both would leave Unit 2's banked increases unused; applying 13.1% to both would exceed Unit 1's allowable amount. sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Banked increases

Raising both units with each unit's own lawful amount is a consistent approach, but it is not a legal safe harbor. Fair housing laws prohibit treating tenants differently because of a protected characteristic. The key is a legitimate, documented, non-discriminatory reason. sf.gov — Housing discrimination; CRD — Housing; HUD — Report Housing Discrimination

DecisionReview pointOfficial source
One unit or bothThe reviewed sources do not require both units to be increased. Check each tenancy's own documented rent history and all applicable conditions; selecting one unit does not by itself establish discrimination.Rent Board — Banked increases; SF — Housing discrimination
Different amountsRecord the neutral reasons, base rents, prior increases and calculations. The proposed banked amounts remain unverified until the histories are checked; a record does not guarantee immunity from challenge.Rent Board — Banked increases
Protected characteristics or retaliationDo not select tenants because of a protected characteristic or retaliate for protected activity.California Civil Rights Department — Housing; Civil Code § 1942.5
CoercionBad-faith efforts to influence a tenant to vacate through fraud, intimidation or coercion are prohibited. Section 37.10B(a)(5) gives an example involving an increase for a unit exempt from rent limits.Rent Ordinance § 37.10B
Do not use a uniform percentage as a shortcut. A uniform percentage is not required by law and may be unlawful if it exceeds a unit's allowable amount. Use each unit's own verified rent history.

What a tenant lawyer will look for

  • Whether the unit is covered and the build year is verified. sf.gov — Rental laws
  • Whether a current rent increase license was on file on the effective date. sf.gov — Housing Inventory
  • Whether the base rent and anniversary date are correct. sf.gov — Rent increases
  • Whether the allowable percentage and banked calculation are correct. sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Whether the notice period and content are correct. Civ. Code §827
  • Whether there is any protected-class discrimination or retaliation. sf.gov — Housing discrimination
  • Whether there are habitability or repair issues that could affect the increase. sf.gov — Tenant petitions
