Rent increase license — consequenceAn annual or banked increase purporting to be effective while the landlord is unlicensed is null and void in its entirety. The effective date, not the service date, controls. Banking rights survive a void increase. Applies to increases effective on/after 2022-07-01 generally, and on/after 2023-03-01 for condos and buildings with fewer than ten units.Rent Board Rules § 13.14; sf.gov — Noncompliance
License — ordinance hook (§ 37.15)§ 37.3(a)(1) grants the annual increase only to a landlord "who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15"; banking under § 37.3(a)(2) is likewise "subject to Section 37.15". § 37.15(c) sets the start dates at July 1, 2022 (10+ non-condo units) and March 1, 2023 (condos and <10 units), with annual updates by March 1 and contact updates within 30 days.Rent Ordinance § 37.3(a)(1)–(2); § 37.15
Notice content — consequenceThe notice must identify which portion is the annual increase and/or banked amount, and any capital-improvement, utility or RAP-loan portion. Retaliation — 180-day barCiv. Code § 1942.5(a): where the tenant is not in default in rent, the landlord may not increase rent within 180 days of a good-faith habitability notice or oral complaint, a recorded agency complaint, a resulting inspection or citation, the filing of a tenantability proceeding, or an adverse judgment/award. The period runs from the latest applicable date. A tenant may invoke it once per 12 months.Civ. Code § 1942.5(a)–(b)
Retaliation — organising§ 1942.5(d) separately makes it unlawful to increase rent to retaliate for lawful participation in a lessees' association or other lawful exercise of rights. Reporting or threatening to report a tenant or their associates to immigration authorities is itself prohibited retaliation.Civ. Code § 1942.5(c)–(e)
Retaliation — good-faith grounds§ 1942.5(g) permits the act if the notice states the ground on which the lessor in good faith seeks to increase rent; if controverted, the lessor must establish its truth at the hearing. § 1942.5(h)–(i) provide actual damages, punitive damages of $100–$2,000 per retaliatory act involving fraud, oppression or malice, and attorney's fees.Civ. Code § 1942.5(g)–(i)

Key takeaway

For these units, the local Rent Ordinance is the operative rent-increase rule if the 1940 build year is confirmed. The 2026–27 allowable increase is 1.6%. Unit 2 may also have banked increases from prior years, which are calculated from the official 571 table and must be verified against the actual rent history. sf.gov — Current Rates; sf.gov — Banked increases

Do not assume coverage: The 1940 build year is owner-supplied. Confirm it on the SF Planning Property Information Map or SF Assessor record before relying on local rent control. SF Planning PIM
