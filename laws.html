Local, state and federal law

The three layers that apply to a 1940 building in ZIP 94122, the controlling provision for each, and which layer wins where they overlap.

Which layer wins

The three layers are not additive. They stack, with the most restrictive applicable provision controlling. For this property the local layer is the most restrictive, so it governs the amount — but the state layer still governs notice and procedure, and the federal layer still governs discrimination.

The provision most often got wrong: AB 1482's 8.8%

Many summaries of California rent law lead with "8.8% for San Francisco". That figure is real — it is the AB 1482 cap for 1 August 2026 to 31 July 2027 [sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection…] — but it does not apply to a unit that is already under a local cap lower than the state cap. Civil Code § 1947.12(d)(3) excludes exactly that case, and the Rent Board says so in terms: the state maximum "does NOT apply for units under the rent increase limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance" [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…][sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection…].

For a 1940 building in ZIP 94122 that is the whole answer on the amount: 8.8% is unavailable, 1.6% is the ceiling, and any notice written to 8.8% would be void.

Two local provisions with real teeth

Algorithmic rent-setting is banned outright

Section 37.10C took effect on 14 October 2024. It is unlawful to sell or to use revenue-management software that sets or recommends rents using competitors' non-public data. Each month of a continuing violation, and each unit affected, is a separate violation, with civil penalties of up to $1,000 and attorneys' fees for a prevailing tenant [sf.gov/information--algorithmic-devices-set-…]. For a two-unit owner the practical effect is simple: there is no compliant way to import a software-generated "market" rent into this analysis.

Charging for an added occupant is an unlawful increase

Section 37.3(a)(11) prohibits raising rent solely because a tenant added an occupant, "notwithstanding a rental agreement or lease that specifically permits a rent increase for additional occupants" [sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations][sf.gov/information--charging-additional-occu…]. Unit 1 added M T. Any part of the Unit 1 increase that is attributable to that fact is unlawful; the increase must rest purely on the anniversary.
