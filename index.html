First question: Rent Board license for an individual owner?

Yes. The Housing Inventory applies to owners of residential property, not only corporations. Reporting a tenant-occupied unit generates a Rent Increase License. A current license must be on file before an annual or banked increase takes effect. Official guidance describes annual Housing Inventory reporting plus a written tenant notice; it does not direct a separate Rent Board filing of each increase notice, and the license need not be attached to the tenant notice. Although the rule keys the license to the effective date rather than expressly to notice service, the prudent course is to verify the license in the Portal before serving notice. sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Rent increases

Time-critical: Unit 2’s service window closes 2026-11-30

Unit 2’s 13.1% requires an effective date on or before 2027-02-28 — the 2026–27 rate expires that day and the 2027–28 percentage is unpublished. A 90-day notice must therefore be served by 2026-11-30 (personal delivery) or 2026-11-25 (mail). Today (2026-11-10) the earliest reachable effective date is 2027-02-08 by personal service, or 2027-02-13 by mail. Unit 1 is not urgent: serve by 2027-01-02 for a 2027-02-01 effective date. Action Checklist · Banking Interval (F-16) · sf.gov — Form 571 · Civ. Code § 827

Watch item: proposed 10% cap on combined banked increases (File 261020) — not enacted

On 2026-11-09 the Rent Board posted a notice on the Current Rates, Banked rent increases and Learn about rent increases pages: the Board of Supervisors is considering File No. 261020, which would temporarily limit combined banked plus capital improvement increases on rent-controlled units to 10% per year for three years. As proposed it would apply to banked increases noticed on or after 2026-09-15. The official Legistar record shows status Pending Committee Action (introduced 2026-09-15; Land Use and Transportation Committee) — it has not been enacted and is subject to change. Nothing changes today: under current law Unit 2’s 13.1% is lawful if the ledger is right. If it were enacted as proposed, 13.1% would exceed the cap and the phased 10.0% option ($315.00 → $3,465.00) would be the lawful path. Check the Legistar status immediately before serving any Unit 2 notice. Legistar — File 261020 · sf.gov — Current Rates (notice posted 2026-11-09) · Flagged for Review (F-27)

Project scope

This dashboard collects, organizes, and analyzes the requirements for raising rent on two rent-controlled units in a 1940 building in San Francisco's Outer Sunset (ZIP 94122). The 1940 build year, lease dates, rents, and occupancy are tenant-provided intake facts and are marked unverified until confirmed against official records. Tenant names and other personal identifiers are withheld; dates and dollar amounts are preserved as provided.

Privacy: No tenant names, initials, or contact details are published. The site uses placeholders only. Personal information is kept internal.
Not legal advice: This is a compliance review based on official sources. The SF Rent Board cannot give legal advice. Consult a tenant lawyer or a Rent Board counselor before serving a notice.
