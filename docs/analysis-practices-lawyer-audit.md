### B. Reasonable & Normal Business Reasons for Rent Increases

1. **Statutory Self-Justifying CPI Adjustment (`SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a)(1)`):** Under San Francisco law, a landlord **does not need to prove financial hardship or file a petition** to take the published annual allowable increase (`1.6%` for `3/1/2026–2/28/2027`). The Rent Ordinance expressly establishes this annual adjustment (60% of regional CPI inflation) to assure landlords "fair and adequate rents" while protecting tenants from excessive hikes ([SF.gov — Rent Board](https://www.sf.gov/departments--rent-board)).
2. **Neutral, Non-Pretextual Cover-Letter Explanation (Best Practice):** Accompanying the formal `Civ. Code § 827` notice with a polite, neutral cover note citing **standard building-wide CPI indexing for ordinary operating costs — property taxes, property/casualty insurance, municipal water/sewer/refuse rates, and preventative maintenance reserves on a 1940 structure** — demonstrates transparent, good-faith, normal business practice with zero retaliatory or discriminatory animus.

## 4. Raising One Unit vs. Raising Both Units — Anti-Discrimination Legal Determination

**Direct Answer:** **Raising BOTH units under a single, uniform, written building-wide policy (taking effect on each unit's respective lawful eligibility date) is the gold-standard approach to prevent discrimination claims or inquiries.**

### Nuanced Legal Analysis Verified Under Federal, State, and SF Law:
1. **Why Raising Both Units Equally Is Best Practice:** Applying the exact published SF Rent Board annual allowable percentage (`1.6%` for effective dates through `February 28, 2027`) across **both Unit 1 and Unit 2** leaves zero room for any allegation of selective targeting based on protected characteristics under the **Federal Fair Housing Act (`42 U.S.C. § 3604(b)`)**, **California FEHA (`Cal. Gov. Code § 12955` — which covers 19+ protected traits with NO duplex exemption)**, or the **Unruh Civil Rights Act (`Cal. Civ. Code § 51`)**. An SF tenant lawyer reviewing a `1.6%` (`$63.92` on Unit 1; `$50.40` on Unit 2) building-wide annual CPI adjustment will immediately recognize it as textbook, non-discriminatory normal business practice.
2. **When Staggered Timing Between Unit 1 and Unit 2 Is Not Only Lawful, But Mandatory:** Uniform policy does **not** mean violating Unit 1's lease calendar!
   - **Unit 2** (`June 1, 2019` start @ `$3,150/mo`, assuming month-to-month) is **immediately eligible** for an adjustment after `30/35` days' notice (for `≤10%`) or `90/95` days' notice (for `>10%`).
   - **Unit 1** (`February 1, 2025` start @ `$3,995/mo`; continuing tenancy `April 1, 2026` @ `$3,995/mo`):
     - *If* the `April 1, 2026` agreement was merely a month-to-month roommate modification with no rent increase since `February 1, 2025`, Unit 1 passed its 12-month mark on `February 1, 2026` and **both Unit 1 and Unit 2 can be noticed simultaneously right now** (e.g., effective `January 1, 2027` or `February 1, 2027` at `1.6%`)!
     - *If* the `April 1, 2026` agreement created a new **12-month fixed-term lease** running through `March 31, 2027`, California contract law (`Civ. Code § 827`) **prohibits** raising Unit 1 until `April 1, 2027`. In that scenario, raising Unit 2 now while scheduling Unit 1 under the *exact same written policy* upon lease expiration (`April 1, 2027`) is **100% lawful and non-discriminatory**, because lease-term expiration is an objective, neutral contractual criterion.
3. **What About Unit 2's Banked Increases (`2020–2025` = `11.5%`)?**
   - While SF law (`SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a)(2)`) legally permits imposing accumulated banked increases on Unit 2 (based on the objective fact that Unit 2 has had 7+ years at `$3,150/mo` vs. Unit 1's 2025 `$3,995/mo` baseline), imposing a sudden `13.1%` (`+$412.65/mo`) single-year jump triggers **90/95-day notice scrutiny** and almost certainly invites a free SF tenant-lawyer audit of every historical notice, Housing Inventory license year, and building maintenance item.
   - **Recommended Defensible Options:**
     - **Option A (Lowest Legal Risk / Highest Tenant Retention — Standard Corporate & Mom-and-Pop Best Practice):** Apply the uniform **`1.6%` current annual allowable increase** to **both units** (`+$63.92/mo` on Unit 1 → `$4,058.92`; `+$50.40/mo` on Unit 2 → `$3,200.40`), while explicitly noting in internal/written records that Unit 2's prior banked percentages (`11.5%`) remain lawfully reserved under `SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a)(2)`.
     - **Option B (Moderate Phased Catch-Up Policy):** Apply a documented, objective building-wide rule: *"All eligible units receive the current SF Rent Board annual CPI adjustment (`1.6%`), plus up to `3.0%–5.0%` per year of verified prior banked adjustments for any tenancy more than 36 months without an adjustment, capped well below `10%` in any 12-month period."* Keeping total Unit 2 adjustment `≤ 10%` (e.g., `1.6%` annual + `3.4%` partial banked = `5.0%` or `+$157.50/mo` → `$3,307.50/mo`, leaving the remaining `8.1%` banked for future years) stays within the 30/35-day notice tier, avoids rent shock, relies on a neutral tenure-based mathematical rule, and passes SF tenant-lawyer scrutiny cleanly.
