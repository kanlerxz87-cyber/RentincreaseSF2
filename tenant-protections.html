Core protections for these units

ProtectionWhat it meansOfficial source
Rent controlLandlord may only increase rent by the Rent Board allowable percentage once every 12 months, unless a petition is approved.sf.gov — Rent increases
Just cause evictionMost covered tenants can only be evicted for an allowed just-cause reason; the landlord must follow the legal process.sf.gov — Evictions
Notice30-day written notice for ≤10%; 90-day written notice for >10%; +5 days if mailed.sf.gov — Rent increases; Civ. Code §827

IssueRuleOfficial source

RetaliationIllegal to retaliate against a tenant for reporting discrimination or asserting rights.HUD — Report Housing Discrimination; CRD — Housing
Rent Board jurisdictionThe Rent Board does not adjudicate discrimination or retaliation; contact the SF Human Rights Commission.sf.gov — About Rent Board
Statutory retaliation windowCiv. Code § 1942.5(a) bars a rent increase within 180 days of the latest of: a good-faith habitability notice or oral complaint; a recorded agency complaint; a resulting inspection or citation; the filing of a tenantability proceeding; or an adverse judgment. The tenant must not be in default in rent, and may invoke it once per 12 months.Civ. Code § 1942.5(a)–(b)
Organising and immigration threatsIncreasing rent to retaliate for lawful tenant organising is separately unlawful. Reporting or threatening to report a tenant, or people known to be associated with the tenant, to immigration authorities is itself prohibited retaliation.Civ. Code § 1942.5(c)–(e)
Tenant remedies for retaliationActual damages; punitive damages of $100–$2,000 per retaliatory act involving fraud, oppression or malice; reasonable attorney's fees to the prevailing party if requested at the outset.Civ. Code § 1942.5(h)–(i)
Algorithmic pricing enforcementA tenant or a tenants' rights organization may bring a civil action over the use of prohibited rent-setting algorithms, with damages, injunctive relief, penalties up to $1,000 per violation and attorney's fees. A lease clause limiting a prevailing tenant's fees is unenforceable.sf.gov — Algorithmic devices prohibited
Void increase for no licenseA tenant can check the Portal for a license on the effective date and, if none existed, file an unlawful rent increase petition — or a summary petition if the increase has not yet taken effect. A refund may be owed across multiple years.sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance; Rules § 13.14
Added-occupant overchargesA tenant who paid extra because an occupant was added may petition to void the increase and recover the overcharges.sf.gov — Additional occupants prohibited

Tenant-lawyer review checklist

A tenant-friendly lawyer would likely review the following before deciding whether an increase is reasonable and lawful. This checklist is based on official sources, not a guarantee of outcome.

Review itemQuestionOfficial source
CoverageIs the unit covered by the SF Rent Ordinance? Confirm the build year and unit type.sf.gov — Rental laws; SF Planning PIM
LicenseWas a current rent increase license on file on the effective date?sf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rent Board Portal
Base rentIs the base rent correct and supported by the lease and rent history?sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Rent increases
Anniversary dateIs the effective date at least 12 months after the last increase or lease start?sf.gov — Rent increases
Allowable percentageIs the percentage in effect on the effective date, not the notice date?sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Current Rates
Banked calculationAre the banked percentages correct, not compounded, and not rounded up? Has each skipped allowance completed its 12-month wait from when it could first have been imposed (Rules § 4.12(a))?sf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — Rule 4.12
NoticeIs the notice written, timely, and does it state the dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and banked portion?sf.gov — Banked increases; Civ. Code §827
New tenant / additional occupantDid a new tenancy begin, or did an additional occupant join a continuing tenancy? Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant.sf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — Rent increases
PassthroughsAre any passthroughs properly calculated, disclosed, and separate from base rent?sf.gov — Landlord petitions
HabitabilityAre there repair or habitability issues that could support a rent reduction or challenge?sf.gov — Tenant petitions
Discrimination / retaliationIs there any evidence of a protected-class motive or retaliation?sf.gov — Housing discrimination; CRD — Housing; HUD
DocumentationAre the lease, rent history, license, notice, proof of service, and calculation preserved?sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Tenant petitions
No guarantee: A tenant lawyer may still challenge an increase even if it appears lawful. The Rent Board cannot give legal advice, and this site does not guarantee that a lawyer will approve the increase. sf.gov — Rent Board counselor
