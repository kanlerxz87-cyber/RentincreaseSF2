License (§37.15)Owners of covered units must report unit information to the Rent Board. A landlord who has substantially complied “shall receive a license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2)”; during noncompliance the license is “temporarily suspended.”sf.gov — §37.15; sf.gov — Housing Inventory
Banking§37.3(a)(2) lets a landlord who refrains from imposing an annual increase accumulate it and impose it on subsequent anniversary dates, subject to §37.15. The Rent Board's published rule is that a skipped increase is banked only if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase; no limit on amount; not compounded; not rounded up; does not carry over to a new tenant.sf.gov — §37.3; sf.gov — Banked increases

State — California

RuleValue / requirementOfficial source
AB 1482 statewide cap5% + regional CPI change, or 10%, whichever is lower, in any 12-month period; no more than two increases in 12 months. The Rent Board states the current maximum for units under AB 1482 in San Francisco is 8.8% (5% + 3.8% CPI) for August 1, 2026 – July 31, 2027.Civ. Code §1947.12; sf.gov — AB 1482
Interaction with local lawCivil Code §1947.12(d)(3) exempts housing already subject to a local ordinance that restricts annual increases to less than 5% + CPI. The Rent Board states AB 1482 “does NOT apply for units under the rent increase limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance.” AB 1482 is a floor, not a ceiling.Civ. Code §1947.12; sf.gov — AB 1482

Key takeaway

For these units, the local Rent Ordinance is the operative rent-increase rule if the 1940 build year is confirmed. The 2026–27 allowable increase is 1.6%. Unit 2 may also have banked increases from prior years, which are calculated from the official 571 table and must be verified against the actual rent history. sf.gov — Current Rates; sf.gov — Banked increases

Do not assume coverage: The 1940 build year is tenant-provided. Confirm it on the SF Planning Property Information Map or SF Assessor record before relying on local rent control. SF Planning PIM
