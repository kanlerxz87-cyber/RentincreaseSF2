Why the anniversary date is the hinge for Unit 1

Rule 1.11(a) defines the anniversary date as the date the tenant's current rent became effective, and says the next increase may not take effect until at least one year later [sf.gov/reports/december-2022/rent-board-rule…]. Two dates are plausible on the facts supplied:

If the tenancy continuedRent of $3,995.00 has been effective since 1 February 2025. The anniversary is 1 February. A 1.4% increase became available on 1 February 2026 and was not taken. The earliest date on which both the banked 1.4% and the current 1.6% can be imposed together is 1 February 2027.
If a new tenancy beganThe new rent of $3,995.00 became effective 1 April 2026. The anniversary is 1 April 2026, and no annual increase may take effect before 1 April 2027 — by which time the rate will be whatever the Board publishes for the period beginning 1 March 2027, not 1.6%.

This is recorded as flag F-02 because the supplied facts support either reading, and the difference is roughly fourteen months of delay. A Rent Board counselor can resolve it.

What would change the answer entirely

Three facts, if true, would move either unit out of the analysis above. Each is worth checking before anything is served.

  • A valid written Section 6.14 notice was served, and every original occupant has since left. That would permit an increase without the annual cap [sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-sec…]. On the facts supplied it has not happened — an original occupant remains — but for Unit 2 it depends entirely on whether E R and I S are both still resident.
  • The unit is a separately-alienable single-family home or condominium and the tenancy began on or after 1 January 1996. That would remove rent control altogether, leaving only AB 1482 and just cause [sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws]. The building is described as having two units, which points away from this, but the building type has not been stated.
  • The unit is not a legal dwelling unit. An unauthorised unit can still be a rental unit under the Ordinance, so this does not remove rent control — but it changes the remedy available and is worth knowing [sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws].
