Owner Practices — Corporate vs. Individual

Same legal rules No official corporate distinction Unverified practice claims

The short answer

Official sources do not create a separate rent-increase rule for big real estate corporations versus individual property owners. Both are "landlords" under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance and must follow the same allowable percentage, license, notice, and anti-discrimination requirements. sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Rent Ordinance

No official source located by this review documents a distinct corporate practice for handling rent increases in San Francisco. Any claim that corporations handle increases differently is unverified and is flagged for review. sf.gov — About Rent Board

Important: The absence of an official corporate-specific rule does not mean a corporation has no compliance obligations. It means the same local, state, and federal rules apply. A tenant lawyer will review the actual notice and calculation, not the owner's corporate form.

Corporate vs. individual owner: what is verified and what is not

QuestionOfficial answerStatusOfficial source
Do corporations have a different rent-increase cap?No. The Rent Ordinance applies to covered units regardless of ownership form.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Rent Ordinance
Do corporations have a different notice requirement?No. The same written notice and 30/90-day rules apply.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; Civ. Code §827
Do corporations have a different license requirement?No. The Housing Inventory and rent increase license requirements apply to owners of covered units.Verifiedsf.gov — Housing Inventory
Do corporations handle increases differently in practice?No official source located. This is not verified.Unverifiedsf.gov — About Rent Board
Do individual owners have a different rule?No. Individual owners follow the same Rent Ordinance.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases
Is there an official definition of "normal business practice"?No official definition located beyond the Rent Board formula and petition process.Unverifiedsf.gov — Rent increases
Verified rule — algorithmic pricingFor every landlord, San Francisco prohibits algorithmic devices that use non-public competitor rent or occupancy data to recommend rents or occupancy. This is not a corporate-only rule, and ordinary unit-management software is expressly not prohibited. For covered devices, each unit and each month is a separate violation, with penalties up to $1,000 per violation and a right of action for tenants and tenants' rights organizations.Verifiedsf.gov — Algorithmic devices prohibited (§ 37.10C)
Verified difference — entity ownershipUnder AB 1482, the single-family-home / condominium exemption is unavailable where the owner is a real estate trust, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. Not operative for these units, which are rent-controlled and outside AB 1482, but it is a genuine corporate/individual distinction in state law.Verifiedsf.gov — AB 1482, exempt housing types
No difference — the licenseThe Housing Inventory and rent increase license apply to all owners of residential property. There is no individual-owner or small-building exemption; buildings under ten units were simply phased in later, on 2023-03-01.Verifiedsf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rules § 13.14
No difference — the percentageThe allowable annual increase, banking rules, notice periods and base-rent definition are identical for corporate and individual owners of covered units.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; Rent Ordinance § 37.3

Practical compliance steps for any owner

StepActionOfficial source
1. Report into Housing InventoryReport the unit and confirm the license is current before the increase takes effect. Best practice is to verify it before serving notice.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rent Board Portal
2. Verify the rent historyConfirm base rent, last increase date, and any prior increases.sf.gov — Banked increases
3. Calculate the allowableUse the official percentage in effect on the effective date; add only properly banked amounts.sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — 571 table
3a. Check current legislationCorporate and individual owners alike must check File 261020 immediately before service. It is Pending Committee Action, not enacted as of 2026-11-11; the Rent Board says the proposed 10% cap would reach banked increases noticed on/after 2026-09-15.sf.gov — proposal warning; Official tracker
4. Serve written noticeInclude dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and banked portion; keep proof of service.sf.gov — Banked increases; Civ. Code §827
5. Document the reasonKeep a written record of the lawful reason for the increase and the calculation.sf.gov — Landlord petitions
6. Review for discriminationApply the same lawful process to similarly situated tenants and avoid protected-class considerations.sf.gov — Housing discrimination; CRD — Housing

Corporate practices: what is and is not established

No official source located in this review establishes what large corporations typically do. Their internal procedures and how often they raise rent are unverified. The same applicable legal rules govern an individual owner; the steps above are a practical checklist, not a documented survey of corporate practice.
