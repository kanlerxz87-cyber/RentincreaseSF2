Executive summary

What the law permits, what it forbids, and the three decisions that remain open.

1. The controlling facts

Two units in one building, constructed in 1940, in the Outer Sunset (ZIP 94122). A building from 1940 is inside the Rent Ordinance's rent-control cutoff of 13 June 1979 [sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws]. Both units are therefore on the local rent ceiling, which is far lower than the state ceiling and which displaces it.

UnitUnit 2 — last lease 1 Jun 2019 at $3,150.00; both original tenants E R and I S remain.

2. The three layers of law, in one line each

Local1.6% for increases effective 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027, once every 12 months, on base rent only, plus properly banked prior years. Non-conforming increases are null and void [sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_…][sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations]
StateAB 1482 would allow 8.8% for San Francisco, but it is expressly switched off where a local ordinance caps increases lower — as San Francisco's does [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]. Civil Code § 827 sets the notice periods: 30 days at 10% or less, 90 days above 10% [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
FederalNo rent ceiling exists in federal law. The Fair Housing Act regulates how rent is set, not how much: differential treatment on the basis of race, colour, religion, sex, familial status, national origin or disability is unlawful [law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604]

3. What each unit may lawfully be charged

Unit 1 — maximum$4,114.85 / month, an increase of $119.85 (3.0%). That is 1.4% banked from the 2025-26 period plus the 1.6% annual increase, taken on the 1 February anniversary. Anything above this without a Rent Board petition is void [sf.gov/reports/december-2022/rent-board-rule…]
Unit 2 — maximum$3,562.65 / month, an increase of $412.65 (13.1%). That is 11.5% banked across six skipped years plus the 1.6% annual increase. Requires 90 days' written notice because it exceeds 10% [sf.gov/reports/december-2022/rent-board-rule…][leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
Do notDo not take a market-rate reset on either unit, do not charge more because Unit 1 gained an occupant, and do not use revenue-management software to pick a number — each is independently unlawful [sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-sec…][sf.gov/information--charging-additional-occu…][sf.gov/information--algorithmic-devices-set-…]

4. The nine findings

  1. The local cap governs, and it is 1.6%. AB 1482's 8.8% is not available to either unit. Any analysis that starts from 8.8% is wrong for this property [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
  2. Neither unit can be reset to market rent. Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins both require the last original occupant to have left. L O remains in Unit 1; E R and I S remain in Unit 2 [sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-sec…][leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
  3. Unit 1 gained an occupant — that is a trap, not an opportunity. Raising rent because M T joined would be an unlawful increase even if the lease permits it [sf.gov/information--charging-additional-occu…]
  4. Unit 2's 13.1% is lawful and smaller than it looks. Six skipped years add to 11.5%. On a $3,150 base that is $412.65. Taken year by year with compounding it would have been $435.72 — the no-compounding rule cuts in the tenant's favour, not against them [sf.gov/reports/december-2022/rent-board-rule…]
  5. Both increases must be itemised. The notice must state which portion is annual, which is banked, and the dates the banked amount rests on. A non-conforming notice voids the whole increase [sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations]
  6. The licence is the gating item. Without a current rent increase licence on the effective date, the notice is unenforceable regardless of the arithmetic [sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory]
  7. Raising both units is not required, but a written policy is. Nothing in Unruh, FEHA or the FHA requires an owner to raise every tenancy by the same amount. What the law forbids is a difference that tracks a protected characteristic. Both units here take the same 1.6% annual increase; the difference is each tenancy's own banked history [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
  8. No justification is needed — and inventing one is risky. An annual allowable increase needs no reason beyond the statute. Citing "market rent" invites a comparable-rent fight in which the Rent Board has already said a long-standing low rent is not an extraordinary circumstance [sf.gov/information--special-circumstances-pe…]
  9. Two files are exposed. The licence, and Unit 1's anniversary date. Both are fixable before any notice is served [sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory][leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]

5. The three open decisions

Decision 1 — what date?Unit 1 needs 1 February 2027 to preserve the banked 1.4%. Unit 2 needs a 90-day runway, so a mid-February 2027 effective date means serving by 17 November 2026. Both dates fall inside the 1.6% period, which ends 28 February 2027. See the Notices page for the full calendar.
Decision 2 — all of it, or part?There is no obligation to take the maximum. Banking has no expiry during a tenancy, so an unused percentage is not lost — it waits. What is lost is any percentage not yet banked when the anniversary date changes [sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases]
Decision 3 — who reviews it?Before serving, put the draft notice in front of a Rent Board counselor (free, 415-252-4600) and, if the banked portion is claimed, a landlord-side attorney. The Lawyer review page runs the twelve checks a tenant-side attorney would run.
The one-sentence answer Raise Unit 1 to $4,114.85 on 1 February 2027 and Unit 2 to $3,562.65 on 15 February 2027 — after confirming the rent increase licence is current and fixing Unit 1's anniversary date — and give Unit 2 ninety days' notice. Anything above those figures requires a Rent Board petition; anything served without a licence is unenforceable.
