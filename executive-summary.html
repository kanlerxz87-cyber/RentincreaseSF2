1. First question: as an individual owner, do I still need the Rent Board license?

Yes. There is no small-owner or individual-owner exemption. The Rent Board states that all owners of residential property must report into the Housing Inventory, and that owners who report a unit as tenant-occupied receive a Rent Increase License for that unit. The requirement turns on the unit being rent-controlled and tenant-occupied — not on whether the owner is a corporation.

Is this building in scope? If the owner-supplied 1940 construction date and other coverage facts are confirmed, yes. The phase-in date depends on facts this review cannot verify. Rent Ordinance § 37.15(a) reaches owners of residential dwelling units subject to Chapter 37. § 37.15(c) then splits the start dates: for units other than condominiums in buildings with ten units or more, reporting began July 1, 2022; for condominium units and units in buildings with fewer than ten units, reporting began March 1, 2023. The intake says this is a two-unit building, which would put it in the second group — but the unit count is owner-supplied, and so is the 1940 build year that establishes rent-control coverage in the first place. Rules § 13.14 then applies the license requirement to increases effective on or after those same two dates: July 1, 2022 generally, and March 1, 2023 for condominium units and buildings with fewer than ten residential units. Those two conditions are tracked as F-31 (number of residential units) and F-01 (build year / coverage). The answer to the licensing question does not change either way: both start dates are long past, there is no small-owner or individual-owner exemption, and a unit that is tenant-occupied and rent-controlled needs a current license whenever the increase takes effect. What the unverified facts decide is which phase-in sentence applies, not whether a license is needed. sf.gov — § 37.15 · Rules § 13.14 · Flagged for Review — F-31, F-01

The coverage condition is separate from the licensing phase-in. The Rent Board states that a unit built before June 14, 1979 has rent control only if it is also not used as a single family dwelling; the build date alone is not the test. A two-unit building satisfies the second condition, because the Board states that a single-family dwelling with a legal or an illegal in-law unit constitutes a two-unit building and is not exempt. So on the supplied facts the units are covered — but both the 1940 build date and the two-unit count remain owner-supplied (F-01, F-31). See F-33. sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws · sf.gov — Costa-Hawkins partial exemption

It is not a separate licence application. Reporting into the Housing Inventory through the Rent Board Portal automatically generates the license for non-owner-occupied units. Reporting is free, online, and available 24/7; the annual deadline is March 1 (and contact changes must be updated within 30 days). Separate from the free Inventory filing, owners also owe the annual Rent Board Fee ($59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026–27; exemption deadline 2026-12-14; due 2027-03-01; 50% / $29.50 recoverable from Nov 1 tenants once paid in full — requesting a fee exemption does not satisfy Inventory reporting).

sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · § 37.15 · Rent Board Rules § 13.14 · sf.gov — Rent Board Fee · Rent Board Portal

2. Must I inform the Rent Board before notifying the tenants?

The formal legal deadline is the effective date of the increase, not the date you serve the notice. Rules § 13.14 says so in terms: "the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used to determine whether or not the increase conforms with the provisions of this section."

There is no separate per-increase filing with the Rent Board for an ordinary annual or banked increase. The official process is (a) annual Housing Inventory reporting, which produces the license, and (b) written notice to the tenant. The license does not have to be attached to the tenant notice. Above-allowance increases are different — those require a petition.

Recommended sequence anyway: complete the Inventory and confirm the license in the Portal before serving. It costs nothing, and it removes the single defect that voids an otherwise perfect increase.

Rules § 13.14 · sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
