Rent Board notice vs. tenant notice

As an individual owner, you are subject to the same Housing Inventory requirement as other residential property owners. Report the unit; a tenant-occupied unit receives a Rent Increase License. The license must be current before the rent increase takes effect. The official guidance describes written notice to the tenant but does not instruct a separate filing of each increase notice with the Rent Board; the license does not need to accompany the tenant notice. While the formal deadline is the effective date, verify the license before serving notice as the lower-risk sequence. sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Rent increases

Correct way to inform tenants

StepRequirementOfficial source
1. Confirm coverageVerify the unit is covered by the SF Rent Ordinance. A 1940 building is covered if the build year is confirmed; check the SF Planning Property Information Map or SF Assessor record.sf.gov — Rental laws; SF Planning PIM
2. Confirm licenseHold a current rent increase license before imposing an annual or banked increase. The license is issued through Housing Inventory reporting.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rent Board Portal
3. Verify base rent and anniversaryUse the tenant's base rent and the date of the last increase (or lease start if no increase). The effective date of an increase more than 12 months later becomes the new anniversary date.sf.gov — Rent increases
4. Calculate the allowableMultiply base rent by the allowable percentage in effect on the effective date. Add only properly banked percentages. Do not compound or round up.sf.gov — formula; sf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — 571 table
5. Choose effective dateCount at least 30 days for ≤10%, or 90 days for >10%, from service. Add 5 days if the notice is mailed. Do not make the increase retroactive without a valid notice.sf.gov — notice; Civ. Code §827
6. Serve written noticeDeliver personally or by mail. The notice must state the dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and which part is banked. Keep proof of service.sf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — Rent increases
7. Keep recordsKeep the lease, rent history, license, notice, proof of service, and calculation. A tenant may challenge the increase at the Rent Board.sf.gov — Tenant petitions; sf.gov — Tenant summary petitions
8. Lawyer reviewHave a tenant lawyer review the calculation, notice, and documentation before service. The Rent Board cannot give legal advice.sf.gov — Tenant and Landlord Resources; sf.gov — Rent Board counselor

One unit or both?

No official source requires raising both units. The Rent Ordinance applies to each unit based on its own base rent and rent history. Unit 1's maximum at the current annual allowance is 1.6%; Unit 2's maximum may be 13.1% if the banked calculation is correct. Applying 1.6% to both would leave Unit 2's banked increases unused; applying 13.1% to both would exceed Unit 1's allowable amount. sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Banked increases

Raising both units with each unit's own lawful amount is a consistent approach, but it is not a legal safe harbor. Fair housing laws prohibit treating tenants differently because of a protected characteristic. The key is a legitimate, documented, non-discriminatory reason. sf.gov — Housing discrimination; CRD — Housing; HUD — Report Housing Discrimination

Do not use a uniform percentage as a shortcut. A uniform percentage is not required by law and may be unlawful if it exceeds a unit's allowable amount. Use each unit's own verified rent history.

What a tenant lawyer will look for

  • Whether the unit is covered and the build year is verified. sf.gov — Rental laws
  • Whether a current rent increase license was on file on the effective date. sf.gov — Housing Inventory
  • Whether the base rent and anniversary date are correct. sf.gov — Rent increases
  • Whether the allowable percentage and banked calculation are correct. sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Whether the notice period and content are correct. Civ. Code §827
  • Whether there is any protected-class discrimination or retaliation. sf.gov — Housing discrimination
  • Whether there are habitability or repair issues that could affect the increase. sf.gov — Tenant petitions

Step-by-step and deadlines

The Rent Board licensing process is set out step by step at the front of the Executive Summary. The license must exist on the effective date; an unlicensed annual or banked increase is null and void in its entirety. Rules § 13.14; sf.gov — Housing Inventory
