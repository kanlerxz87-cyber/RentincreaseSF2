Who reviews, and at what cost A tenant can challenge an increase through the Rent Board at no cost, with no time limit, and without a lawyer [sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-requ…]. What the two blocking items mean in practice

Item 2 — the rent increase licence

A landlord must hold a current rent increase licence before imposing an annual or banked increase, and the licence must be current on the effective date of the notice, not the service date [sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory]. The licence is generated automatically once the Housing Inventory submission is made, so the fix is administrative and quick — but it must happen before the notice, not after.

Item 7 — the itemised breakdown

Section 37.3(b) requires the landlord to tell the tenant in writing which portion of the increase is the annual increase and which is banked [sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations]. Rule 4.12(b) adds the base dates for the banked amount, and says omission does not by itself void the increase [sf.gov/reports/december-2022/rent-board-rule…]. The two readings sit awkwardly together, and the safe construction is to comply with both. On Unit 2 the banked portion is six separate percentages across six years, which is precisely the notice a careless owner writes as "13.1% banked increase". Use the template on the Notices page.

The test that is not on the list, and matters most

Every check above is about compliance. The one a lawyer runs first is different: "is there anything in the file that shows the owner knew the increase was wrong?" That is what turns a clerical error into a penalty argument. On this file the answer should be no — but only if the owner does not, for example, serve 30 days' notice on a 13.1% increase because a 90-day deadline was missed, or describe a banked increase as "market adjustment". Both are recorded risks, at F-06 and on the Reasons page respectively.
