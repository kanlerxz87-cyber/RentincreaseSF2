Decision rule: verify first; choose second; notice last

This page separates questions the owner can decide from facts that must be established first. It does not choose a rent increase for the owner or certify that any figure is lawful. The base rents, rental histories, household facts, coverage, unit count and current license status have not been independently verified. Do not treat a computed scenario as authorization to serve a notice.

For an ordinary annual or banked increase, first report the units in the Housing Inventory and confirm the automatic license is current for the intended effective date. The Rent Board's stated legal test is that the license be current when the increase takes effect; serving after checking the Portal is the prudent sequence. There is no separate Rent Board filing for each ordinary increase. sf.gov — Housing Inventory and license · Rent Board Rules § 13.14

Fairness and independent review

Do not increase both units merely to make the decision look nondiscriminatory. No official rule located requires matching increases across different units. Use each tenant's verified base rent, individual anniversary and prior increase history, apply the same objective criteria, document the legitimate reason and timing, and check for protected activity or protected-class concerns. Different lawful outcomes can result from different histories; identical amounts are not a safe harbor.

There is no official measure of a "normal" increase for Outer Sunset ZIP 94122 beyond the applicable lawful formula and any properly approved petition/passthrough. Market-rent comparisons do not set the annual allowable increase for a covered unit. The Rent Board can explain its process but does not provide legal advice. A tenant-side legal-service provider represents eligible tenants, not this owner; for owner-side pre-service review, ask a private landlord-tenant attorney or a Rent Board counselor about the applicable procedure. No lawyer's approval or outcome can be guaranteed.
