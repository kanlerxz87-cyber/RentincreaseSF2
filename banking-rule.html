Banking Interval (F-16)

Banking Interval — Resolving Flag F-16

Independent re-verification F-16 resolved on the evidence Service window closes 30 Nov 2026 Not legal advice

What this page adds

The main site is correct and comprehensive. This page does three things it does not do:

  1. Resolves flag F-16. Two official Rent Board sources state different banking intervals — Rules §4.12(a) says 12 months, a summary page says 24. F-16 records the conflict as open. It is not actually open: the Rent Board has published two worked examples, and only one of the three possible readings reproduces both of them. That is a decisive test, and it is set out in section 2 below.
  2. Independently re-derives every published figure from primary sources, without reference to the site's own arithmetic. Section 6 records the result, claim by claim.
  3. States the service window as a deadline. The site records the window's outer limit as a fact. This page converts it into the dates notice must actually be served by, counted back from today, 10 November 2026. Section 5.

No figure on the main site is contradicted by this page. The audit that produced it is at AUDIT.md.

1. The three candidate readings

The sentence in dispute is on the summary page: "A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date." It admits of two readings, and the regulation supplies a third. sf.gov — Banked rent increases

ReadingTest appliedSourceOfficial source
R1 — per-skipped-allowance, 12 monthsA skipped allowance banks once a full 12 months have elapsed between the date it first became available and the date of the new increase.Rent Board Rules and Regulations §4.12(a)sf.gov — Rules Part IV
R2 — global, 24 monthsOnce 24 months have passed with no rent increase at all, every skipped allowance is banked. One clock, measured from move-in or the last increase.Summary page, read literallysf.gov — Banked rent increases
R3 — per-skipped-allowance, 24 monthsEach skipped allowance separately needs 24 months from its own availability date.Summary page, read per allowancesf.gov — Banked rent increases

The ordinance itself is silent on the interval. Rent Ordinance §37.3(a)(2) grants the banking right — "A landlord who refrains from imposing an annual rent increase or any portion thereof may accumulate said increase and, subject to Section 37.15, impose that amount on the tenant's subsequent rent increase anniversary dates" — but sets no waiting period. The interval comes only from Rules §4.12. So the regulation is the controlling text, and R1 is its plain meaning: "A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable." sf.gov — Sec. 37.3; sf.gov — Rule 4.12(a)

F-16 resolved. R1 — the per-allowance 12-month test in Rules §4.12(a) — is the only reading consistent with both official worked examples. R2 contradicts Example B; R3 contradicts both. The primary regulation, the flagship guidance page's worked example, and the summary page's own worked example all agree under R1.

Why the summary page still reads as 24 months. Its sentence is best understood as describing the ordinary single-skip case, where 24 months from move-in is the same moment as 12 months from the first availability date. That equivalence holds for the first skipped allowance only, and breaks down once there are several — which is exactly Example B. The sentence is imprecise rather than wrong in a different direction.

Recommended action: F-16 can be closed as resolved on the evidence, with R1 recorded as the operative rule and this test cited. A one-line confirmation from a Rent Board counselor remains worthwhile before serving a notice above 10%, because the counselor can confirm the Board's current practice and the Rent Board cannot give legal advice. sf.gov — Speak with a Rent Board counselor

The binding constraint is Unit 2. A 13.1% increase needs 90 days' notice, and 13.1% cannot be priced beyond 28 February 2027 until the Rent Board publishes the 2027-28 percentage. That leaves a service window of roughly 10 to 30 November 2026 for personal delivery — driving the opening back to 20 days. Serving by mail removes the first five days of it, and a mailed notice targeting the earliest date is already out of time. Banked amounts may only be given at the time of an annual increase, so the banked 11.5% cannot be imposed on its own later. sf.gov — Form 571; sf.gov — Rule 4.12(a)

7. Two further primary-source points worth recording

PointVerified textWhy it matters hereOfficial source
The license test is applied to the effective date, not the service date"Any rent increase under Ordinance Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2) purporting to be effective while the landlord is unlicensed shall render the entire rent increase null and void; provided, however, that the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used to determine whether or not the increase conforms."Confirms the sequencing advice on the main site and pins the test to a date the owner controls. A license obtained between service and the effective date is sufficient.sfrb.org — Rules §13.14; sf.gov — Form 538 worksheet (same rule restated)
A void increase does not destroy banking rights"Nothing in this Regulation shall affect a landlord's banking rights or the ability to accumulate otherwise lawful rent increases that were found to be null and void."Materially reduces the downside of the F-03 license gap: a notice that fails for want of a license loses that increase, not the accumulated 11.5%. It is still a failure, and the tenant may file a summary petition, but the banked balance survives.sfrb.org — Rules §13.14; sf.gov — Tenant summary petitions
The license arises from substantial compliance with reporting"A landlord who has substantially complied with their obligation to report information about a rental unit as described above shall receive a license to increase rent for that unit under Ordinance Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2)." (Adopted July 12, 2022)Answers the individual-owner question at the level of the regulation: the trigger is reporting the unit, not the owner's corporate form.sfrb.org — Rules §13.13/§13.14; sf.gov — Sec. 37.3(a)(1)
Non-conforming increases are void under the ordinance itselfRent Ordinance §37.3(b)(5): "Nonconforming Rent Increases. Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void."The void consequence comes from the ordinance as well as from Rules §4.10(b) and §13.14, so it is not a technicality that can be argued away.sf.gov — Sec. 37.3(b)(5)
New discrepancy found: the license requirement's start dates differ between official sourcesThe Housing Inventory page and the rent-increase page give 1 July 2022 for buildings of 10+ units and 1 March 2023 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. The Rent Board's own Form 538 worksheet states the requirement applies to increases effective on or after 1 July 2020 (10+ units) and 1 March 2021 (fewer than 10 units).Low practical impact — both dates are long past and a current license is required now either way — but it is an inconsistency between two official Rent Board documents and is logged as irregularity A-06 in AUDIT.md. For a two-unit building the relevant group is "fewer than 10 units" on either version.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Get a rent increase license; sf.gov — Form 538 worksheet
