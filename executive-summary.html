Bottom line (as of November 6, 2026)

  • Annual allowable increase (Outer Sunset 94122 pre-1979 rent control): 1.6% effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 (superseding California AB 1482's 8.8% SF County cap). sf.gov — Current Rates; oag.ca.gov — Rent Caps
  • Unit 1 (Tenant 1A continuing original tenant + Tenant 1D; Feb 1, 2025 lease @ $3,995; Apr 1, 2026 continuing tenancy @ $3,995): Because original occupant Tenant 1A still resides in Unit 1, Costa-Hawkins / Rules §6.14 market reset is barred. If month-to-month, the maximum prospective increase prior to Feb 1, 2027 is 1.6% = +$63.92/month (new rent $4,058.92). Waiting until February 1, 2027 (24 full months from Feb 1, 2025) banks 1.4% + 1.6% = 3.0% (+$119.85/month → $4,114.85). First confirm the April 1, 2026 occupancy document did not create an unexpired 12-month fixed term through March 31, 2027. sf.gov — Rules §6.14; sf.gov — Irregular periods
  • Unit 2 (Tenant 2A & Tenant 2B; June 1, 2019 lease @ $3,150): Standard current-year 1.6% = +$50.40/month (new rent $3,200.40, 30/35-day notice). If zero increases were imposed since 2019 and the same tenants remain, banked allowances (2020–2025 = 11.5%) plus current 1.6% = 13.1% = +$412.65/month (new rent $3,562.65, 90/95-day notice). Calendar warning: 95 days from Nov 6, 2026 is Feb 9, 2027 (≤2/28/2027); delaying the effective date into March 1–May 31, 2027 forfeits June 1, 2026's 1.6% under Topic 503. sf.gov — 571 table; sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Notice & mail service: Written notice at least 30 days (≤10%) or 90 days (>10%) before the prospective effective date, plus +5 calendar days if mailed within California (CCP §1013). Never backdate an unserved increase. Civ. Code §827; CCP §1013
  • Mandatory Housing Inventory license: A current Rent Increase License under SF Admin. Code §37.15 must be active on the effective date, or the increase is null and void (43 tenant summary petitions filed in FY24–25). sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Noncompliance
  • Corporate vs. individual practices: All landlords face identical Ch. 37 caps, licensing, and notice rules. Corporate landlords rely on standardized Rent Board forms (Forms 516D/538/542) and multi-unit passthrough petitions, while SF Admin. Code §37.10C (Ord. 207-24) and U.S. DOJ enforcement strictly outlaw algorithmic rent-setting software. sf.gov — Forms Center; sf.gov — Algorithmic ban
  • One unit or both & Fair Housing: No statute mandates identical percentages across units with different histories, but FEHA (Gov. Code §12955 — no duplex exemption for non-owner-occupied 2-unit buildings), Unruh (§51), FHA (42 U.S.C. §3604), and SF law bar arbitrary or protected-class disparate treatment. Adopting a single written, non-discriminatory building policy applied consistently to both units on their lawful eligibility dates is the cleanest compliance posture. Gov. Code §12955; HUD — FHA Overview
  • Tenant-lawyer scrutiny: In ZIP 94122, tenants filed 42 Rent Board petitions in FY24–25 and have access to free counseling/legal clinics. Pre-auditing license status, lease term, math truncation (never rounding up), mail days, habitability, and 180-day anti-retaliation (Civ. Code §1942.5) minimizes challenge risk, though lawyer approval can never be guaranteed in advance. sf.gov — FY24–25 Report; Civ. Code §1942.5

What is "reasonable and normal"?

The official standard for a rent-controlled unit is the Rent Board's annual allowable increase, not a market-rate or "reasonable rent" opinion. For 2026–27, that is 1.6%. sf.gov — Current Rates The annual allowable is intended to cover ordinary operating-cost increases; it is the normal, no-petition increase. sf.gov — Rent increases

Unit 2's 13.1% is not the "normal annual" amount. It is a one-time catch-up of skipped annual allowances (11.5%) plus the current annual allowance (1.6%). It is lawful only if the banked calculation is correct, the license is current, and the 90-day notice is given. sf.gov — Banked increases A tenant-friendly lawyer may scrutinize a seven-year catch-up even if it is technically lawful.

No official definition: Official sources do not define "reasonable" or "normal business practice" beyond the Rent Board formula. A tenant lawyer's approval cannot be guaranteed. sf.gov — Rent Board counselor
