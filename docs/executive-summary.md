## 1. Key Legal Conclusions at a Glance

1. **What Is a Reasonable & Normal Rent Increase in Outer Sunset (`94122`)?**
   - Because the two-unit building was constructed in **1940** (prior to the **June 13, 1979** certificate-of-occupancy cutoff), **both units are strictly governed by San Francisco Rent Control (`SF Admin. Code § 37.3`)**, which supersedes California's looser AB 1482 cap (`8.8%` in SF County for `8/1/2026–7/31/2027`).
   - For any rent increase taking effect between **March 1, 2026 and February 28, 2027**, the official SF Rent Board **Annual Allowable Rent Increase is `1.6%`** (calculated at 60% of Bay Area CPI-U inflation).
   - **Unit 1 (`$3,995.00/mo` base rent):** Standard `1.6%` annual adjustment = **`+$63.92/mo`** → **`$4,058.92/mo`** (note: because original occupant `Tenant 1A` from the `February 1, 2025` lease remains in occupancy alongside replacement co-occupant `Tenant 1D` as of `April 1, 2026`, Costa-Hawkins and `Rules § 6.14` **prohibit** any market-rate reset; verify whether `April 1, 2026` created a new 12-month fixed term before serving notice).
   - **Unit 2 (`$3,150.00/mo` base rent since `June 1, 2019`):** Standard `1.6%` annual adjustment = **`+$50.40/mo`** → **`$3,200.40/mo`**. *(If zero increases were imposed since 2019, `11.5%` in skipped 2020–2025 annual increases — `$362.25/mo` — is banked under `SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a)(2)`, for a theoretical `13.1%` / `+$412.65/mo` ceiling through `2/28/2027` requiring a 90/95-day notice; however, applying the uniform `1.6%` annual increase or a modest `≤5%` phased catch-up while preserving remaining banked rights is far safer under SF tenant-lawyer scrutiny.)*

2. **Corporate vs. Individual Property Owner Practices in San Francisco:**
   - **Corporate / Institutional Landlords** apply automated, calendar-driven annual CPI increases (`1.6%`) to **every** eligible unit on its exact anniversary date, maintain active `portal.sfrb.org` **Rent Increase Licenses (`§ 37.15`)**, utilize formal Rent Board **Capital Improvement / Utility / Bond Passthrough** worksheets (`15` Capital Improvement petitions filed in ZIP `94122` in FY 24–25), serve `Rules § 6.14` notices on every new roommate within 60 days, and avoid algorithmic rent-setting tools banned by **`SF Admin. Code § 37.10C` (Oct 2024)** and prosecuted by the **U.S. DOJ**.
   - **Individual ("Mom-and-Pop") Owners** frequently forego increases for years (accumulating banked balances), overlook the mandatory **March 1, 2023 Housing Inventory Rent Increase License** (which renders unlicensed increases **null and void** under `Rules § 13.14`), round up dollar amounts, or serve notices informally by email/text — errors routinely overturned by SF tenant attorneys.

3. **Correct Way to Inform Tenants & Reasonable Reasons:**
   - First confirm an active **SF Rent Board Rent Increase License** on `https://portal.sfrb.org` for both units and confirm zero open habitability/repair disputes (`Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5`).
   - Serve a formal **written** Notice of Change in Terms of Tenancy (`Cal. Civ. Code § 827(b)`) via **personal delivery** or **USPS first-class mail (`CCP § 1013` adds +5 calendar days)**: **30 days** (`35` if mailed) for increases `≤ 10%`; **90 days** (`95` if mailed) for increases `> 10%`.
   - State current base rent, exact unrounded dollar increase, exact percentage (plus banked breakdown if any), and effective date. The published SF Rent Board CPI adjustment requires **no petition or hardship proof** and is universally recognized as normal business practice offsetting rising property taxes, insurance, utilities, and maintenance costs.

4. **One Unit vs. Both Units — Passing SF Tenant Lawyer & Fair Housing Review:**
   - **Raise BOTH units under a single, written, uniform Building-Wide Annual Rent Adjustment Policy** (taking effect on each unit's lawful eligibility/lease-expiration date).
   - Uniformly applying the published `1.6%` SF Rent Board CPI rate to both Unit 1 and Unit 2 eliminates disparate-treatment inquiries under the **Federal Fair Housing Act (`42 U.S.C. § 3604(b)`)**, **California FEHA (`Cal. Gov. Code § 12955`)**, and **Unruh Civil Rights Act (`Cal. Civ. Code § 51`)**, and cleanly passes line-by-line review by any SF tenant advocate or Tenant Right to Counsel attorney.
