Rent Board, license, and process

#QuestionAnswerStatusOfficial sourceDetail
1As an individual property owner (not a corporation), do I still need to inform the Rent Board and hold a license before notifying tenants of a rent increase?Yes. All owners of residential property must report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory, and a current rent increase license must be on file on the effective date of any annual allowable or banked increase. There is no exemption for individual owners.Verifiedsf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — §37.15; sf.gov — §37.3Rent Board Process & License
2What is the step-by-step process?Confirm coverage → report into Housing Inventory by March 1 → confirm the automatic rent increase license → pay the separate Rent Board Fee → establish base rent and anniversary date → calculate → choose an effective date at least 30/90 days out → serve written notice → keep records → optional lawyer review.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Housing InventoryRent Board Process & License
3Does holding the license prevent the increase from being challenged?No. It removes the “unlicensed increase” ground (which can make the increase null and void), but it is not Rent Board approval. A tenant may still file an unlawful rent increase petition or a tenant summary petition, and there is no filing charge.Verifiedsf.gov — Noncompliance notice; sf.gov — Tenant petitionsRent Board Process & License
4Do I have to file a Rent Board petition for an ordinary annual or banked increase?No. “Landlords are not required to file a petition with the City to impose these types of rent increases.” Petitions are only for above-cap claims.Verifiedsf.gov — Multiple years; sf.gov — Landlord petitionsNotice & Process

What is a reasonable and normal increase?

#QuestionAnswerStatusOfficial sourceDetail
5What is a reasonable and normal increase under San Francisco (local) law?The Rent Board's annual allowable increase: 1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027, imposed once every 12 months on the tenant's anniversary date, on base rent only, with no compounding and no rounding up.Verifiedsf.gov — Current Rates; sf.gov — Rent increasesLaws & Regulations
6What about state law (California)?AB 1482 caps increases at 5% + regional CPI, or 10%, whichever is lower. The Rent Board states the current maximum for units under AB 1482 in San Francisco is 8.8% (5% + 3.8% CPI) for August 1, 2026 – July 31, 2027. It does not apply to units already under the SF Rent Ordinance's rent increase limitations.VerifiedCiv. Code §1947.12; sf.gov — AB 1482Laws & Regulations
7What about federal law?There is no federal rent cap. Federal law (the Fair Housing Act) prohibits discrimination in the terms, conditions, or privileges of rental based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability.VerifiedHUD — Report Housing Discrimination; 42 U.S.C. Chapter 45Laws & Regulations
8Which law controls for these units?If the 1940 build year is confirmed, the local Rent Ordinance controls, because Civil Code §1947.12(d)(3) exempts housing already subject to a local ordinance that restricts increases to less than 5% + CPI, and the Rent Board states AB 1482 does not apply to units under the local rent increase limitations.VerifiedCiv. Code §1947.12; sf.gov — AB 1482Laws & Regulations
9What is the maximum lawful increase for Unit 1?1.6% of base rent = $63.92 on a $3,995 base, for a new rent of $4,058.92. The 1.4% (2025–26) increase is not banked yet because less than 24 full months have passed without an increase. Waiting until February 1, 2027 allows 1.4% + 1.6% = 3.0% = $119.85, new rent $4,114.85.Calculated from unverified base rentsf.gov — formula; sf.gov — Banked increasesIncrease Analysis
10What is the maximum lawful increase for Unit 2?If no prior increase was ever imposed and the same two tenants remain, the banked total is 11.5% plus the current 1.6% = 13.1% = $412.65 on a $3,150 base, for a new rent of $3,562.65. This is a calculated maximum, not a verified current rent; any prior increase changes it.Calculated from unverified base rentsf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — 571 tableIncrease Analysis
11Is there an official definition of “reasonable” or “normal business practice”?No. Official sources define the allowable percentage and the petition process; they do not define a separate reasonableness or business-practice standard. Anything beyond that is opinion.Unverifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — About Rent BoardIncrease Analysis

How owners handle increases

#QuestionAnswerStatusOfficial sourceDetail
12How do big real estate corporations handle rental increases?No official source located documents a distinct corporate practice. Corporations are subject to the same allowable percentage, the same Housing Inventory and license requirement, the same notice rules, the same petition process, and the same fair housing laws as any other owner.Unverified practice claimsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — §37.15Owner Practices
13How do individual property owners handle increases in San Francisco?Through the same Rent Ordinance process: report into Housing Inventory, hold the license, apply the allowable percentage (plus properly banked amounts) to base rent on the anniversary date, and give 30/90-day written notice.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Housing InventoryOwner Practices
14Does corporate ownership change the cap, the notice, or the license requirement?No. Each of those requirements is stated in terms of “a landlord” or “owners” and applies to covered units regardless of ownership form.Verifiedsf.gov — §37.3; sf.gov — §37.15Owner Practices

Notice and reasons

#QuestionAnswerStatusOfficial sourceDetail
15What is the correct way to inform tenants?Written notice delivered personally or by mail, at least 30 days before the effective date (90 days if the increase, alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months, is more than 10%), plus 5 more days if mailed. The notice should state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which part is banked. Keep proof of service.Verifiedsf.gov — notice; sf.gov — Banked increases; Civ. Code §827Notice & Process
16What are reasonable reasons for the increase?The annual allowable increase; properly banked increases; or, only with a Rent Board petition filed before the notice, capital improvements, operating & maintenance expenses, special circumstances, utility passthroughs, or bond passthroughs.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Landlord petitionsNotice & Process
17Can I charge more rent because a new roommate moved in?No. “A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge.”Verifiedsf.gov — Additional occupantsNotice & Process
18Can the increase be backdated?No. A rent increase takes effect only after a valid notice has been given for the required period. A notice cannot be backdated.Analysis; lawyer reviewsf.gov — notice; Civ. Code §827Rent Board Process & License

Tenant-lawyer scrutiny and fairness

#QuestionAnswerStatusOfficial sourceDetail
19Can the increase be made to pass review by an SF tenant lawyer as normal business practice with no discrimination or broken laws?Following the Rent Board formula exactly, holding a current license, giving correct notice, and documenting the reason makes the increase lawful on its face. No one can guarantee a lawyer's approval in advance: the Rent Board itself states it cannot give legal advice, and a tenant may still petition. “Normal business practice” has no official definition beyond the formula.Not guaranteedsf.gov — Rent Board counselor; sf.gov — Tenant petitions; sf.gov — Tenant and Landlord ResourcesTenant Protections
20Can tenants get free tenant lawyers in San Francisco?Yes, several organizations provide free or income-restricted legal help and tenant counseling. Eligibility and capacity vary and must be confirmed with each organization. Note that Open Door Legal's published service area is ZIP codes 94107, 94112, 94124, and 94134 — not 94122.Verifiedsf.gov — Tenant and Landlord Resources; sf.gov — Legal resources for housing discriminationTenant Protections
21Would raising one unit or both units avoid a discrimination inquiry?Neither choice is a legal safe harbor. There is no official requirement to raise both units, and each unit has its own base rent and rent history, so a uniform percentage is not required and may be unlawful for one of the units. What matters is applying each unit's own lawful formula and having a legitimate, documented, non-discriminatory reason.Analysis; lawyer reviewsf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Housing discriminationIncrease Analysis
22Is a rent increase based on a protected characteristic illegal?Yes. San Francisco prohibits changing the terms of housing based on a protected class, and California and federal law prohibit different terms or conditions of rental based on a protected characteristic. Retaliation for asserting rights is also prohibited.Verifiedsf.gov — Housing discrimination; CRD — Housing; HUDTenant Protections
23Does the Rent Board decide discrimination or retaliation claims?No. The Rent Board does not adjudicate harassment, discrimination, or retaliation; those go to the San Francisco Human Rights Commission, the California Civil Rights Department, or HUD.Verifiedsf.gov — About Rent BoardTenant Protections
