Reasonable reasons for a rent increase

What may be said, what should not be said, and the exact statutory language that carries the least risk.

The short version For an annual allowable increase you do not need a reason. The Ordinance supplies it: the Board publishes a percentage each year and a licensed landlord may impose it once every 12 months [sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations]. Providing a reason you do not need creates a factual claim that can be tested. The safest notice states the statutory basis and stops there.

Reasons to avoid — and why each backfires

"Market rent is $5,200"Invites the comparable-rent framework, where the Board has already held that a long-standing below-market rent is not an extraordinary circumstance [sf.gov/information--special-circumstances-pe…]. It also concedes that the increase is market-driven, which it cannot lawfully be for a covered unit.
"Your unit is bigger / has a view"Units are already differently priced. A differential for amenities invites a housing-services argument rather than supporting the increase.
"You added a roommate"Unlawful to charge for additional occupants [sf.gov/information--charging-additional-occu…]
"Our costs went up"Costs are not the basis for an annual increase — they require a separate petition with proof. Asserting them in the notice creates an evidentiary burden the owner then has to meet [sf.gov/information/topic-no-322-operating-an…]
"The other tenant is paying more"Puts the comparison in writing and invites the discrimination frame. The lawful comparison is to the statutory schedule, not to another tenancy.
"Because you complained" / anything tied to conductRetaliation, separately unlawful and void [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]

The recommended wording

One sentence, which cannot be contradicted by anything else in the file:

This increase is the annual allowable increase published by the San
Francisco Rent Board for increases effective between 1 March 2026
and 28 February 2027 (1.6%), together with the banked increases
attributable to this tenancy under Section 37.3(a)(2) and Rent Board
Rules and Regulations Section 4.12.

And if the owner wants to say why, in a covering letter rather than the statutory notice:

We review rent once a year, on each tenancy's anniversary date, and
apply the increase the Rent Board publishes for that year, together
with any increase that was available in a previous year and not
taken. This is applied the same way to every tenancy we manage.

That second paragraph is doing real work: it is a written, contemporaneous statement of a uniform policy, which is the single most useful document if a discrimination question is ever raised on the one-unit-or-both question.

When a reason genuinely is required

Above the capA petition, with evidence, heard by an Administrative Law Judge [sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs]
Operating and maintenanceComparison-year versus base-year actual costs, with documentation [sf.gov/information/topic-no-322-operating-an…]
Capital improvementCertification of the work, cost, useful life and amortisation [sf.gov/apply-raise-rents-capital-improvement…]
Comparable rentsEvidence of genuinely comparable units and of the extraordinary circumstance that caused the low rent [sf.gov/information--special-circumstances-pe…]

None of these is needed for either unit on the figures recommended here. Both increases are ordinary annual-plus-banked increases.
