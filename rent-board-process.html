Rent Board Process & License — Step by Step

Individual owners included Official ordinance text License status unchecked Not legal advice

The question

“I read that I need to inform the Rent Board and apply for a license in order to prevent the rent increase from being challenged. I am just an individual property owner and not part of a corporation, so do I still need to apply for the license and inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants of a rent increase?”

Short answer: Yes. Being an individual owner — not a corporation — does not exempt you. Under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance, every owner of a rent-controlled unit must report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and hold a current rent increase license before an annual allowable or banked rent increase can take effect. The requirement attaches to the covered unit, not to the owner's corporate form.

Verified answer — the official text

Official sourceWhat it saysLink
Rent Ordinance §37.15(e) — Reporting Obligations; Licensing“If a landlord has substantially complied with the obligation to report information about a rental unit as required under this Section 37.15, the landlord shall receive a license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2). If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance. Upon receipt of the required information from the landlord, the suspension shall be terminated, and the license to impose rent increases shall be restored prospectively, but a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.”sf.gov — §37.15
Rent Ordinance §37.3(a)(1) — Rent Limitations“A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60% of said published increase. In no event, however, shall the allowable annual increase be greater than 7%.”sf.gov — §37.3
SF Rent Board — Housing Inventory“All owners of residential property are required to report.” / “If your unit is covered under rent control, you will need to comply with the Housing Inventory requirements before any annual or banked rent increase notice goes into effect.” / “Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license that allows them to impose annual allowable and banked rent increases.”sf.gov — Housing Inventory
SF Rent Board — Learn about rent increases“A landlord must get a rent increase license before imposing annual allowable and/or banked rent increases on a tenant. To get a rent increase license, a landlord must first report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory. This is effective July 1, 2022 (or March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with 1-9 residential units).”sf.gov — Rent increases
SF Rent Board — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory“A rent increase notice that goes into effect while the unit is unlicensed may be null and void (this may apply for multiple years)” / “If the unit is rent-controlled and you issued a rent increase that went into effect when no license was on record, your tenant may be entitled to a rent refund.”sf.gov — Noncompliance notice
Does the license “prevent a challenge”? No. The license removes one of the most common grounds for a successful tenant challenge (an unlicensed increase can be null and void), but it is not Rent Board approval of your increase. A tenant may still file an Unlawful Rent Increase petition or a Tenant Summary Petition, and the Rent Board will review the calculation, notice, and rent history.

What the license is — and what it is not

QuestionAnswer from official sourcesOfficial source
Do I have to “apply” for it?There is no separate application. “Once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.”sf.gov — Housing Inventory
Do individual owners need it?Yes. The Housing Inventory requirement applies to “all owners of residential property,” and §37.15(e) grants the license to “a landlord” who has substantially complied with reporting. No source located creates an exception for individual (non-corporate) owners.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — §37.15
Does the license authorize an above-cap increase?No. It covers only “annual allowable and banked rent increases.” Anything more requires a landlord petition filed before the notice is served.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Landlord petitions
Must the license be attached to the notice?No. “The rent increase license does not need to be included with your rent increase notice.”sf.gov — Housing Inventory
When must it be on file?On the effective date of the increase. “A landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect.”sf.gov — Housing Inventory
Is reporting the same as paying the Rent Board Fee?No. “Reporting into the Rent Board Housing Inventory does not request an exemption of the Rent Board Fee.” The fee is a separate annual obligation.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Rent Board Fee
What if I file after the March 1 deadline?“You can still make a Housing Inventory submission and receive a rent increase license after the deadline. However, your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice.” The Rent Board does not assess monetary penalties for filing late.sf.gov — Housing Inventory
How do I check my status?Search the address or Assessor's Parcel Number (APN) in the Rent Board Portal and view the property. You may need to check multiple years for all relevant dates.Rent Board Portal; sf.gov — Noncompliance notice

Report each unit into the Housing InventoryReport the mailing address, owner-occupied status, owner/manager business contact information, business registration number, approximate square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy/vacancy dates, and base rent in $250 increments (including whether utilities are included).Annually by March 1. 4. Confirm the rent increase licenseOnce a tenant-occupied unit is reported, the license is generated automatically. Verify it in the Portal and check the status and expiry for the year of your effective date.Must be current on the effective date of the increase.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rent Board Portal
5. Pay the separate annual Rent Board FeePay through the Portal. For the 2026–2027 tax year the fee is $59.00 per dwelling unit ($29.50 per guest unit). Once paid in full, you may recover 50% ($29.50 per dwelling unit) from tenants in occupancy as of November 1 of the tax year.Due March 1 each year; 5% late penalty per month after that, capped at 15%.sf.gov — Rent Board Fee
6. Establish the base rent and anniversary dateThe anniversary date is the day/month of the last rent increase, or the day/month the lease began if the rent has never been increased. Calculate on base rent only (parking and storage are part of base rent; passthroughs are not).Base rent and history must be supported by the lease and your records.sf.gov — Rent increases
7. Calculate the allowable amountMultiply base rent by the percentage in effect on the effective date (not the notice date). Add only properly banked percentages. Increases cannot be compounded, prorated, or rounded up.Once every 12 months only. 1.6% is the allowable for 3/1/2026–2/28/2027.sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Banked increases
8. Choose a lawful effective dateCount at least 30 days from service if the increase is 10% or less, or 90 days if it is more than 10% (alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months). Add 5 days if the notice is mailed. The increase cannot be made retroactive without a valid notice.Do not use a date that has already passed.sf.gov — notice; Civ. Code §827
9. Serve the written noticeDeliver personally or by mail. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which portion is banked. Keep proof of service.30 / 90 days before the effective date, plus 5 days if mailed.sf.gov — Banked increases; Civ. Code §827
10. Keep the fileKeep the lease, rent history, Housing Inventory confirmation, license, notice, proof of service, and the calculation worksheet. A tenant may challenge the increase at the Rent Board, and there is no filing charge for a tenant petition.Ongoing.sf.gov — Tenant petitions
11. Optional: independent reviewHave a tenant lawyer review the calculation, notice, and documentation. The Rent Board provides free counseling but expressly cannot give legal advice.Before serving the notice.sf.gov — Rent Board counselor; sf.gov — Tenant and Landlord Resources
12. Only if you need more than the allowable/banked amountFile the applicable landlord petition (capital improvement, operating & maintenance, special circumstances, utility passthrough, bond passthrough) before serving the notice. Tenants are not required to pay the increase until the Rent Board approves it.Petition first, notice second. There is no filing charge.sf.gov — Landlord petitions

What happens if the license is not on file

ConsequenceOfficial source
The increase notice that went into effect while the unit was unlicensed “may be null and void,” and this “may apply for multiple years.”sf.gov — Noncompliance notice
The tenant “may be entitled to a rent refund” for the unlicensed period.sf.gov — Noncompliance notice
The Rent Board may send tenants a Notice of Owner Noncompliance telling them you are not in compliance.sf.gov — Noncompliance notice
Under §37.15(e), the license is “temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance,” is restored only prospectively, and the tenant is not obligated to pay the increased rent for the months of suspension.sf.gov — §37.15
A tenant can file an Unlawful Rent Increase petition (no filing charge) or, if the increase has not yet taken effect, a Tenant Summary Petition.sf.gov — Tenant petitions; sf.gov — Tenant summary petitions

When the license is not required (verified exceptions)

SituationOfficial source
“The Rent Increase License is not required in some situations where the original occupant(s) no longer live in the unit” — that is, rent increases under Rules and Regulations Section 6.14 and/or the Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act.sf.gov — Noncompliance notice; sf.gov — §6.14 and Costa-Hawkins
Passthroughs allowed under the Rent Ordinance (general obligation bond, water revenue bond, and certain utility passthroughs) do not require the rent increase license.sf.gov — Noncompliance notice; sf.gov — Landlord petitions
Units that are not covered by San Francisco rent control. The Rent Board states: “The Rent Increase License rules apply ONLY to units under San Francisco Rent Control.”sf.gov — Noncompliance notice
Relevant to Unit 1: Unit 1's intake facts say one original tenant continues and one additional occupant joined. Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins apply when the last original occupant vacates. Because an original tenant continues, do not assume a market-rate increase is available. A landlord is not required to file a petition for a §6.14/Costa-Hawkins increase but may do so, and a tenant or subtenant may file a petition alleging the increase is not warranted. sf.gov — §6.14 and Costa-Hawkins

Corporate vs. individual owner — the direct answer

No official source located creates a different Housing Inventory, license, percentage, notice, or petition rule for corporations versus individual owners. The Rent Ordinance speaks about “a landlord” and “owners of residential dwelling units,” and the Housing Inventory page says “all owners of residential property are required to report.” sf.gov — §37.15; sf.gov — Housing Inventory

Practical implication: As an individual owner you have exactly the same obligations as a large landlord for these units. A tenant lawyer reviewing your notice will check the calculation, the license, the notice, and the rent history — not your corporate form. See Owner Practices.
