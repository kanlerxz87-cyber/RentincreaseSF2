Owner Practices — Corporate vs. Individual

Same Ch. 37 statutory cap SF §37.10C algorithmic ban verified SF Rent Board FY24–25 petition stats Owner-type CPI share unverified

The verified legal and operational picture

Under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance (Administrative Code Chapter 37), big real estate corporations and individual ("mom-and-pop") property owners are bound by the exact same statutory rent-increase caps, Housing Inventory licensing prerequisites, and written notice rules. sf.gov — Rent increases; SF Admin. Code Ch. 37

At the same time, official municipal and federal government sources document three verified operational and enforcement realities separating large institutional portfolios from small individual owners: (1) standardized institutional use of Rent Board passthrough petitions and notice forms across multi-unit buildings, (2) San Francisco's 2024 statutory ban on corporate algorithmic rent-setting software (SF Admin. Code §37.10C, Ord. 207-24), and (3) U.S. Department of Justice antitrust lawsuits and consent decrees against RealPage and six major corporate landlords. Meanwhile, the exact percentage of corporate versus individual SF landlords who impose the annual CPI adjustment each year is not separately tabulated in official Rent Board statistics and is left blank and marked unverified below. sf.gov — Algorithmic pricing ban; sf.gov — FY24–25 Statistical Report; DOJ — RealPage Consent Decree

Compliance takeaway for a 2-unit individual owner: Adopt corporate-grade procedural discipline — verify active §37.15 licensing on portal.sfrb.org, use official Rent Board Form 516D itemizing exact dollar and percentage components, truncate fractions (never round up), add +5 calendar days when mailing (CCP §1013), and never use prohibited algorithmic pricing tools.
