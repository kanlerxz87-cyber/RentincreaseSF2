## Legal Framework & Allowable Increases
### 1. Local Law (San Francisco Rent Ordinance, Chapter 37)
- **Coverage**: Units built before June 13, 1979 (1940 building qualifies) [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Allowable Annual Increase**:
  - March 1, 2025 – February 28, 2026: **1.4%** [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
  - March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027: **1.6%** [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Calculation**: 60% of Bay Area CPI change (capped) [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Notice**: 30 days for increases under 10% [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)

### 2. State Law (California AB 1482 - Tenant Protection Act)
- **Applicability**: Backstop for units not covered by local ordinance; however, SF local ordinance (1.4%/1.6%) is stricter than AB 1482 cap (5% + CPI, max 10%) [2](https://calandlordtenantlaws.com/rent-control-ab-1482)
- **Formula**: 5% + Regional CPI, capped at 10% [2](https://calandlordtenantlaws.com/rent-control-ab-1482)
- **Notice**: 30 days (≤10%) or 90 days (>10%) [2](https://calandlordtenantlaws.com/rent-control-ab-1482)

### 3. Federal Law (Fair Housing Act)
- **No Federal Rent Cap**: Federal law does not set percentage limits [3](https://commoner-law.com/rights/housing-rights/rent-control)
- **Anti-Discrimination**: Prohibits increases based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability [3](https://commoner-law.com/rights/housing-rights/rent-control)
- **Retaliation**: Prohibits increases in retaliation for exercising legal rights [3](https://commoner-law.com/rights/housing-rights/rent-control)

## Analysis & Findings
### Reasonable and Normal Increases
- **Unit 1 (Current $3995)**:
  - 1.4% (2025-26): $55.93/month → New Rent: $4050.93
  - 1.6% (2026-27): $63.92/month → New Rent: $4058.92
- **Unit 2 (Current $3150)**:
  - 1.4% (2025-26): $44.10/month → New Rent: $3194.10
  - 1.6% (2026-27): $50.40/month → New Rent: $3200.40
- **Banked Increases**: Landlords may bank increases for up to 2 prior years (max 3 years total) [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)

### Corporate vs Individual Owner Practices
- **Big Real Estate Corporations**: Typically follow strict SF Rent Board guidelines, use standardized notice forms, and may petition for capital improvement increases [Research Required - see research/practices.md]
- **Individual Owners**: Often handle increases manually, may rely on SF Rent Board counseling, and must ensure compliance with just-cause eviction protections [Research Required - see research/practices.md]

### Tenant Communication & Reasonable Reasons
- **Correct Method**: Written notice (30 days for <10%) using SF Rent Board approved forms [1](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Reasonable Reasons**: Inflation (CPI-based), capital improvements (petition required), operating cost increases, property tax changes [Research Required - see research/communication.md]
- **SF Tenant Lawyer Scrutiny**: Increases must pass review for discrimination, retaliation, and compliance with 1.4%/1.6% caps [Research Required - see research/communication.md]

### Discrimination Analysis (Single vs Dual Unit Increase)
- **Recommendation**: Raise both units consistently to avoid discrimination claims under Fair Housing Act [3](https://commoner-law.com/rights/housing-rights/rent-control)
- **Risk**: Raising only one unit may trigger scrutiny if tenants are in protected classes or if the reason is not documented as unit-specific (e.g., unique capital improvement) [3](https://commoner-law.com/rights/housing-rights/rent-control)
- **Consistency**: Applying the same percentage (1.6%) to both units is the safest defense against discrimination lawsuits [3](https://commoner-law.com/rights/housing-rights/rent-control)
