The answer, before the detail

Both flats are almost certainly covered by the San Francisco Rent Ordinance — the building is from 1940, well before the 13 June 1979 cutoff. That means the annual increase is capped by the Rent Board's published figure, not by market rent. For the current period that figure is 1.6%. AB 1482's higher state cap of 8.8% is switched off precisely because the local cap is lower [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]. No federal statute sets a rent ceiling at all.

Neither unit is eligible for the vacancy reset. Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins only unlock an unlimited increase when the last original occupant has left. An original occupant remains in both units, so the annual cap continues to run in both.

The lawful maximums are therefore narrow and mechanical: Unit 1 may go to $4,114.85 (3.0% = one banked year plus this year's 1.6%), and Unit 2 to $3,562.65 (13.1% = six banked years plus this year's 1.6%). Unit 2's figure is large only because six years of small increases were never taken — the percentages are added, never compounded, exactly as Rule 4.12 requires.

Two things are not yet safe. There is no confirmed current rent increase licence for either unit, and without one a notice is unenforceable no matter how correct the arithmetic. And Unit 1's anniversary date is genuinely ambiguous: if the April 2026 paperwork created a new tenancy, no increase can take effect before April 2027. Both are on the Flagged for Review page with the action needed to close them.

How this site was built — and its limits

What was done

  • Every statutory figure was read from the government page that publishes it, not from a law firm blog or a property manager's calculator.
  • All arithmetic is generated by script from the published table — no number on this site was typed by hand.
  • Where two official pages appear to differ, both are shown and the reconciliation is written out on the Verification log.
  • Where no official source exists, the value is left blank and flagged. HUD's Fair Market Rents are one example.

What this is not

  • It is not legal advice and cannot replace a Rent Board counselor's view on a specific tenancy.
  • It is not an audit of the owner's records. Rent histories, licence status and occupancy are as supplied, not as verified.
  • It does not predict outcomes. It states what the published rules require and where a file is currently exposed.
  • It is current to 6 November 2026. The allowable rate changes each 1 March.
Not legal advice. This site organises publicly available official sources so a property owner can check their own numbers. It is a research aid, not a legal opinion. Rent Ordinance questions should be put to a San Francisco Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) and, for a tenancy-specific opinion, to a licensed attorney.
