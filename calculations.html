Two checks that always catch a bad calculation

Adding, not compounding, works in the tenant's favour

If Unit 2 had taken each increase as it arose, compounding would have produced $3,585.72 by now — $23.07 more than the banked method's $3,562.65. The no-compounding rule is therefore not a penalty on the tenant; it is the reason the banked total is the lower of the two lawful routes. Both are shown so the difference is visible rather than asserted.

Nothing is rounded up

Both results land exactly on the cent: $3,995.00 × 3.0% = $119.85 exactly, and $3,150.00 × 13.1% = $412.65 exactly. No rounding is needed, so the "cannot be rounded up" rule [sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-franci…] is not engaged. If a future calculation produces a fraction of a cent, the standard tolerance is 0.5% of the prior base rent before an increase is voided rather than corrected [sf.gov/reports/december-2022/rent-board-rule…].

The figure that would void everything Any percentage above these totals is not a large increase — it is a null and void increase under § 37.3(b)(5) [sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations], recoverable by the tenant through a free Rent Board petition with no filing fee and no time limit [sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-requ…]. If a higher figure is genuinely needed, the route is a petition, not a notice [sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs].
