How to read this page

Every date below is computed from 2026-11-10, the date of this review, using the official notice periods. The arithmetic is reproducible: run python3 scripts/verify_calculations.py. The inputs — base rents, lease dates and rent history — are owner-supplied and unverified. If any input changes, every date and dollar figure below changes with it.

Gate 0 — prerequisites that must close before any notice is served

#StepWhy it blocks everything elseOfficial source
0.1Report the units into the Rent Board Housing Inventory at the Rent Board Portal and confirm a Rent Increase License exists for each tenant-occupied unit.An annual or banked increase that purports to take effect while the landlord is unlicensed is null and void in its entirety. For a building with fewer than ten units this applies to increases effective on or after 2023-03-01.Rent Board Rules § 13.14 · sf.gov — Housing Inventory
0.2Check the license status for the intended effective date, not just today. Search the address or APN in the Portal.The rule is keyed to the effective date, not the service date. The annual reporting deadline is March 1; a late submission still produces a license, but the increase is unenforceable if no license is on file when it takes effect.Rules § 13.14 · sf.gov — Noncompliance
0.3Confirm the building's construction date / first certificate of occupancy.Rent control and eviction protection generally attach to units built on or before 1979-06-13. A 1940 building would be covered, but the year is owner-supplied and unconfirmed.sf.gov — Rental laws · SF Property Information Map
0.3bConfirm the total number of residential units in the building (F-31).The intake supplies two units, not independent proof of the building total. The count and condominium status determine whether the 2022-07-01 or 2023-03-01 reporting phase-in applied; both dates are now past, so a covered tenant-occupied unit needs a current license either way.sf.gov — § 37.15(a), (c) · SF Planning PIM · SF Assessor
0.3aCheck File 261020 immediately before service.It is Pending Committee Action, not enacted as of 2026-11-10. Proposed § 37.3(h) (Leg Ver1) would bar banked (§ 37.3(a)(2)) and/or capital-improvement (§ 37.3(a)(3)) increases causing rent to rise >10% of lowest prior-12-month base rent through 2029-12-31 (excess deferred to 2030-01-01+), retroactive to banked notices given on/after 2026-09-15, and would add § 37.15(b)(2)(F) banked-increase reporting. Unit 2's 13.1% is a current-law scenario, not immunity from enactment.Official tracker; Leg Ver1; sf.gov — proposal notice
0.4Pull the complete rent ledger for both units: every increase, its date and its percentage.The banked balance is entirely determined by which increases were actually imposed. Unit 2's 13.1% assumes none were. One prior increase invalidates the figure.sf.gov — Banked increases
0.5Run the retaliation screen for each household: any repair request, habitability complaint, agency complaint, inspection, citation or tenant-organising activity.Civil Code § 1942.5(a) bars a rent increase within 180 days of the latest listed event where the tenant is not in default in rent. § 1942.5(d) separately bars an increase made to retaliate for lawful organising.Civ. Code § 1942.5
0.5aFor a Unit 2 off-anniversary scenario using banked amounts, resolve F-28 with a Rent Board counselor or counsel before service.Rules § 4.12(a) says a banked amount is given at the time of an annual increase; current guidance also permits another time when rent may be raised. The scenario pairs banked 11.5% with current annual 1.6%, but the wording difference is not a legal clearance. The conservative date is the 2027-06-01 anniversary; its new annual rate and dollar total remain blank.Rules § 4.12(a) · sf.gov — Irregular periods · F-28
0.6Run the fair housing screen: write down the neutral, unit-specific reason for each percentage before serving anything.Changing the terms of housing because of a protected characteristic is prohibited. San Francisco's list includes age, source of income, family status, pregnancy status, disability and more.sf.gov — Housing discrimination · CA Civil Rights Department
0.7Confirm the building’s total residential unit count and that no coverage exception applies.The licensing tier turns on it: the Rent Board states the law became operative 2022-07-01 for units (other than condominiums) in buildings with 10 or more units, and reporting began 2023-03-01 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. The Rent Board also lists units with eviction protection but no rent control, and eight categories exempt from the Ordinance altogether. The intake names two units but does not establish the building total. (F-01, F-31)sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Which rental laws apply
Privacy note on Gate 0: the Rent Board states that information entered in the Housing Inventory is public, including the owner's name and business contact information, and that submissions are made under penalty of perjury. That public filing is a legal obligation; this website deliberately publishes none of it. sf.gov — Housing Inventory
