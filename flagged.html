F-02HighUnit 1 — is this a continuing tenancy or a new tenancy as of 1 April 2026?
The prompt calls 1 April 2026 a 'continuing tenancy' at the same $3,995.00. But the occupants changed (V N and JP left, M T joined) and a new term apparently began. If a NEW tenancy was created on 1 April 2026, the anniversary date is 1 April 2026 and no annual increase may take effect before 1 April 2027 — which would make scenario B unavailable. If the tenancy continued with L O in place, the anniversary is 1 February and scenario B is available. Costa-Hawkins § 1954.53(d)(3) does not itself reset the rate while an original occupant remains, but the parties' own agreement can create a new term.		Ask the Rent Board counselor to fix the anniversary date. Do not serve a notice on Unit 1 until it is fixed.[leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
F-03HighUnit 2 — confirm both original tenants are still in occupancy
The analysis assumes E R and I S are both still resident and are the original tenants from 1 June 2019. If both original occupants had left and a 6.14 notice was duly served, an unlimited increase would be available instead; if a 6.14 notice was never served, the annual cap continues to apply.		Confirm occupancy. Check whether any written 6.14 notice was ever served. A 6.14 notice cannot be served retroactively.[sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-sec…]

Log into the Rent Board Portal, confirm the Housing Inventory filing is current for both units, and download or record the licence.[sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory]
F-05MediumCoverage not confirmed at parcel level
Built 1940 passes the date test, but a pre-1979 building can still contain units that are exempt (for example a separately-alienable single-family home or condo where the tenancy began on or after 1 January 1996). The building type has not been supplied.		Look the property up on the SF Property Information Map and confirm the number of units and the assessor's use code.[sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws]
F-06LowUnit 2's increase crosses the 10% threshold
13.1% is more than 10% of the lowest rent charged in the prior 12 months, so it requires 90 days' written notice rather than 30. A 90-day deadline is easy to miss and, once missed, pushes the effective date out by months.		Serve by 17 November 2026 for a 15 February 2027 effective date, or re-plan the effective date.[leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displ…]
F-07LowUnit 1 may have already forfeited the 1.4% increase
The 1.4% increase became available on 1 February 2026. Under Rule 4.12 it is only banked once 12 months have elapsed from that date, and under the Rent Board's plain-language statement once 24 months have passed without any increase. Both tests mature on 1 February 2027. Any effective date before then loses the 1.4% permanently.		If the 1.4% matters, set the Unit 1 effective date at 1 February 2027 — not earlier.[sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases]
F-08LowHUD Fair Market Rent figures could not be retrieved
HUD's FY2026 Fair Market Rent documentation system returned null values for San Francisco County when queried. No FMR figure is published on this site rather than risk an unverified number. FMR is in any event a federal subsidy benchmark, not a rent-control ceiling, so it does not affect either unit's lawful rent.		If the figure is needed, read it directly from the HUD FMR dataset file linked in the source register.[huduser.gov/portal/datasets/fmr.html]

The Rent Board publishes eviction, buyout and petition statistics, and monthly filing volumes [sf.gov/information--rent-board-statistics-an…], but no distribution of rent increases taken. Privacy handling

What is publishedInitials only (L O, V N, J P, M T, E R, I S), the year of construction, the ZIP code, tenancy start dates, and dollar amounts.
What is withheldFull names, street address, unit numbers, telephone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, employers, income, and any document that would identify a household.
Why dates and amounts are keptThey are the operative facts. The entire analysis turns on 1 February 2025, 1 June 2019, $3,995.00 and $3,150.00. Redacting them would make the site useless, and they do not by themselves identify anyone.
Residual riskA rent history is effectively unique. Anyone with access to the Rent Board portal and a known address could match these figures to a unit. That is inherent in publishing a real analysis and is disclosed here rather than hidden.
RemovalAny request to remove or further generalise a fact should be honoured by editing tools/_rows.py and re-running tools/build.py.
Not legal advice. This site organises publicly available official sources so a property owner can check their own numbers. It is a research aid, not a legal opinion. Rent Ordinance questions should be put to a San Francisco Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) and, for a tenancy-specific opinion, to a licensed attorney. Every figure is linked to the agency that publishes it; anything that could not be confirmed is listed on the Flagged for Review page rather than guessed.
