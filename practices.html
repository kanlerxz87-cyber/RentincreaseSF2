Owner Practices — Corporate vs. Individual

Same legal rules No official corporate distinction Unverified practice claims

The short answer

Official sources do not create a separate rent-increase rule for big real estate corporations versus individual property owners. Both are "landlords" under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance and must follow the same allowable percentage, license, notice, and anti-discrimination requirements. sf.gov — Rent increases; sf.gov — Rent Ordinance

No official source located by this review documents a distinct corporate practice for handling rent increases in San Francisco. Any claim that corporations handle increases differently is unverified and is flagged for review. sf.gov — About Rent Board

Important: The absence of an official corporate-specific rule does not mean a corporation has no compliance obligations. It means the same local, state, and federal rules apply. A tenant lawyer will review the actual notice and calculation, not the owner's corporate form.

Do individual owners have a different rule?No. Individual owners follow the same Rent Ordinance.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases

Verified difference — algorithmic pricingSan Francisco bans algorithmic revenue-management rent setting outright for every landlord. This is the one place where a large corporate workflow is expressly prohibited rather than merely regulated: each unit and each month is a separate violation, with penalties up to $1,000 per violation and a right of action for tenants and tenants' rights organizations.Verifiedsf.gov — Algorithmic devices prohibited (§ 37.10C)
Verified difference — entity ownershipUnder AB 1482, the single-family-home / condominium exemption is unavailable where the owner is a real estate trust, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. Not operative for these units, which are rent-controlled and outside AB 1482, but it is a genuine corporate/individual distinction in state law.Verifiedsf.gov — AB 1482, exempt housing types
No difference — the licenseThe Housing Inventory and rent increase license apply to all owners of residential property. There is no individual-owner or small-building exemption; buildings under ten units were simply phased in later, on 2023-03-01.Verifiedsf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rules § 13.14
No difference — the percentageThe allowable annual increase, banking rules, notice periods and base-rent definition are identical for corporate and individual owners of covered units.Verifiedsf.gov — Rent increases; Rent Ordinance § 37.3

How large real estate corporations actually handle an annual increase

No official source describes a corporate “method” for raising rent, so nothing below is a claim about corporate practice. What the official sources do describe is the procedure a professional operator follows, and it is the same procedure available to an individual owner. It is reproduced here only because it shows the sequence a tenant lawyer will expect to see in the file.

Professional practice stepWhat the official source requiresOfficial source
Keep a per-unit rent ledgerThe banked balance is determined entirely by which increases were actually imposed; banked percentages are added, never compounded or prorated, and do not carry over to a new tenant. An operator can produce the ledger on request; an owner who cannot produce one cannot prove the banked amount.sf.gov — Banked increases; Rules § 4.12
Report into the Housing Inventory before the effective dateA license is generated automatically for a tenant-occupied unit; it must be current when the increase takes effect. No separate application is filed for each increase.sf.gov — Housing Inventory; Rules § 13.14
Apply the published formula onlyPercentage in effect on the effective date, applied to base rent only, no rounding up, no passthroughs folded into base rent.sf.gov — Rent increases
Do not substitute market data for the formulaRevenue-management software that sets or recommends rent from non-public competitor data is prohibited outright; each unit and each month is a separate violation.sf.gov — § 37.10C
Serve a compliant written notice and keep proof of serviceDollar amount, percentage, effective date, banked portion, and the dates the banked amount is based on; 30 days if the increase is 10% or less, 90 days if more, plus 5 days if mailed.sf.gov — Banked increases; Civ. Code § 827
Apply the same rule to every similarly situated tenantChanging the terms of housing because of a protected characteristic is prohibited; the neutral rule is the published formula, not a target percentage.sf.gov — Housing discrimination; CRD — Housing
Escalate to a petition rather than exceed the capCapital improvement, operating and maintenance, special circumstances, utility and bond passthroughs each have a petition or worksheet that must be filed before the notice is served. A notice served before the petition is void.sf.gov — Landlord petitions
What an individual owner does differently in practice. The obligations are identical; only the paperwork burden differs. A corporation typically has a property manager, a ledger system and counsel on retainer. An individual owner has to build that record personally — the rent ledger, the license confirmation, the notice, and the proof of service — and can get free help with the procedure from a Rent Board counselor (the Board cannot give legal advice) or paid help from counsel. Working from a smaller file is not a defence, so the record is the part an individual owner should not skip. Practice observation — no official source states that corporate and individual owners behave differently
