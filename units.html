Important notes

  • Unit 1: No increase has been reported served, so the anniversary date is the lease start, February 1. The 1.4% allowance that became available on 2026-02-01 becomes banked on 2027-02-01 — a full 12 months after it could first have been imposed, which is also 24 full months after move-in. Rules § 4.12(a); sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Unit 1 recommended: An increase effective 2027-02-01 allows 1.4% banked + 1.6% current = 3.0%, or $119.85, new rent $4,114.85. Taking it earlier yields only 1.6% and the 1.4% is permanently lost. sf.gov — Irregular periods; sf.gov — Rent increases, worked examples
  • Unit 2: The 13.1% total is a conditional calculated scenario, not a verified current rent or legal clearance. It assumes no prior increases and the same tenants, and remains subject to coverage, license, retaliation/discrimination and F-28 review. The conservative annual-only scenario is 1.6% = $50.40 → $3,200.40, leaving 11.5% banked. sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Unit 2 notice: Because 13.1% exceeds 10%, the notice must be at least 90 days, plus 5 days if mailed. Civ. Code §827
  • Additional occupant: A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new roommate moves in with an existing tenant — it is an unlawful increase even if the lease permits it. See Occupancy & Roommates. sf.gov — Additional occupants prohibited
  • New tenant / banked increases: Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant. On the supplied facts Unit 1 is a continuing tenancy because an original occupant remains, so no market-rate reset is available. sf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — Rules 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins
  • Deadlines: See Action Checklist for the computed service dates and Increase Analysis for every effective-date scenario. Civ. Code §827
