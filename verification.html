Verification log

How each figure and each legal conclusion on this site was checked, what it was checked against, and where a check produced a contradiction or a dead end.

Method Each claim was traced to the page published by the agency that owns it. Where two official pages appeared to conflict, both were kept and the reconciliation written out (V-03, V-04). Where a check failed, the value was removed rather than estimated (V-11). Every arithmetic result was produced by script from the published table, so no percentage or dollar figure on this site was typed by hand.

Rule 4.12 says 12 months; the Board's plain-language page says 24 months		Rule 4.12(a) requires a full 12 months to elapse from the date an increase could have been imposed. What verification does not cover

The log verifies the law and the arithmetic. It does not verify the owner's own records: that no increases were taken, that the stated rents are the actual rents of record, that a licence exists, or that the occupants named are the occupants of record. Those sit on the Flagged for Review page because only the owner, or the Rent Board's own file, can confirm them.

This distinction is the reason the site separates "the law says 13.1% is lawful" from "you may lawfully send this notice tomorrow". The first is verified. The second is not, until F-01 to F-04 are closed.

Not legal advice. This site organises publicly available official sources so a property owner can check their own numbers. It is a research aid, not a legal opinion. Rent Ordinance questions should be put to a San Francisco Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) and, for a tenancy-specific opinion, to a licensed attorney. Every figure is linked to the agency that publishes it; anything that could not be confirmed is listed on the Flagged for Review page rather than guessed.
