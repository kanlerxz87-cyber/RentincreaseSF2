There is no single "maximum" — the answer depends on the effective date

The allowable amount changes with when the increase takes effect, because the current annual percentage changes every March 1 and a skipped allowance only becomes banked after a full 12 months have passed from the date it could first have been imposed. Every scenario below is therefore stated with an explicit effective date. All figures are computed from 2026-11-10 and are reproducible with python3 scripts/verify_calculations.py.

sf.gov — Rent increases (use the percentage in effect on the date of the increase) · Rules § 4.12 · sf.gov — Irregular periods

The Unit 2 window closes on 2026-11-30. A 13.1% increase needs a 90-day notice, and the 1.6% annual component can only be used for an effective date on or before 2027-02-28 — the last day of the published rate table. Serving any effective date inside that window therefore requires service by 2026-11-30 by personal delivery, or 2026-11-25 if mailed. Today (2026-11-10) the earliest reachable effective date is 2027-02-08 by personal service, or 2027-02-13 if served by mail. Unit 1 has no equivalent pressure: its best date is 2027-02-01 and notice is due by 2027-01-02. Civ. Code § 827(b)(3)(A) · sf.gov — 571 table · Banking Interval (F-16)

The 12-month / 24-month question — resolved, not left in conflict

Two official pages describe the banking wait in different words. They are consistent once the starting point is identified, and both produce the same dates for these units.

SourceWhat it saysMeasured fromOfficial source
Rent Board Rules § 4.12(a)"A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable."From the date the allowance first became availableRules § 4.12(a)
sf.gov — Banked rent increases"A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase."From the last increase or move-in, which is 12 months earlier than the date the allowance became availablesf.gov — Banked increases
Worked example — Unit 1Tenancy 2025-02-01 → allowance available 2026-02-01 → banked 2027-02-01. That is both 12 months from availability and 24 months from move-in.Both readings agreesf.gov — Rent increases, worked examples
Rent Ordinance § 37.3(a)(2)The ordinance itself states the banking right and the license condition but sets no waiting period; the interval is supplied by Rules § 4.12.—Rent Ordinance § 37.3(a)(2)

F-16 is resolved — only the per-allowance 12-month test in Rules § 4.12(a) reproduces both official worked examples (6.7% and 3.3%); a global-24 reading gives 4.7% against an official 3.3%, and a per-allowance-24 reading gives 5.0% against an official 6.7%. See Banking Interval (F-16) for the decisive test and the change log.

Answer to the question as asked: raising both units is not a legal safe harbour and is not required. Discrimination law prohibits different treatment because of a protected characteristic; it does not require identical percentages. The protection comes from a documented, neutral, unit-specific reason — not from equalising numbers. sf.gov — Housing discrimination · CA Civil Rights Department · HUD

Calculation rules that invalidate an otherwise plausible number

  • No rounding up to the nearest dollar. sf.gov — Rent increases
  • No compounding. Banked percentages are added, then applied once to the current base rent. sf.gov — Banked increases
  • Use the rate in effect on the effective date, not the date the notice is served. sf.gov — Rent increases
  • Base rent only. Parking and storage are generally part of base rent; temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges are not. sf.gov — Irregular periods
  • Once every 12 months from the last increase or the move-in date. sf.gov — Irregular periods
  • A current license on the effective date, or the entire increase is null and void. Rules § 13.14
  • A non-conforming notice can render the entire increase null and void. Rules § 4.10(b)
