Increase Scenarios

Every lawful route for each unit, with percentages, dollar amounts, notice tiers and serve-by deadlines.

How these figures were produced Percentages are the official SF Rent Board allowable annual increases for the period containing each anniversary date (Info Sheet 571). Banked percentages are ADDED, never compounded, and never rounded up (Rent Board Rules & Regulations sec. 4.12). Dollar amounts = base rent x total percent, truncated to the cent as computed. Notice tier from Cal. Civ. Code sec. 827(b): 10% or less -> 30 days; more than 10% -> 90 days; +5 days if served by mail. Every dollar amount and every serve-by date on this page is recomputed by build.py at build time from the official percentages and cross-checked against the stored values; a mismatch fails the build. No figure was typed by hand.
Official rule that drives every scenario below SF.gov's own worked example states that where an owner imposes an increase before a skipped increase has had 12 full months to elapse, that skipped increase 'is permanently lost due to the change in anniversary date'. Timing therefore has real, permanent dollar consequences. sf.gov worked examples

NOT AVAILABLE. Rent Board Rules sec. 6.14 permits an unlimited increase only when the LAST ORIGINAL OCCUPANT vacates. Tenant A is an original occupant and remains, so sec. 6.14 does not apply. Costa-Hawkins (Civ. Code sec. 1954.53(d)) likewise does not apply, and never applies to a co-tenant. Charging more because an occupant was added is expressly an UNLAWFUL rent increase under Admin. Code sec. 37.3(a)(11)(A) and Rent Board Topic 152 — even if the lease allows it.

The Unit 1 decision Waiting until 2027-02-01 instead of acting now yields $119.85 rather than $63.92 per month — a difference of $55.93 per month, $671.16 per year, permanently — and keeps the anniversary date on February 1 so no future percentage is at risk. There is no lawful way to reach market rent on Unit 1 while an original occupant remains.

SF.gov states a landlord need not impose the maximum: any unused portion banks and may be imposed after at least 12 more months. A 10.0% step avoids the 90-day notice and materially softens the impact on a household already paying above the ZIP median. Remaining entitlement is preserved.

The Unit 2 decision All three routes are lawful. U2-A maximises recovery and preserves the June 1 anniversary, but depends on a percentage the Rent Board has not yet published (Flag F-18) and is exposed to proposed banking legislation (Flag F-16). U2-C at exactly 10.0% needs only 30-day notice, preserves the remainder as banked, and is the least likely to provoke a Rent Board petition given that 42.1% of ZIP 94122 renter households are already cost-burdened at 30% or more of income.
