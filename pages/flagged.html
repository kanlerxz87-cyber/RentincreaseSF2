F-03
HIGH
 Banking: 12 months or 24 months?
CONFLICT BETWEEN TWO OFFICIAL SF.GOV SOURCES
Finding. The SF.gov 'Banked rent increases' page states: 'A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date.' The official Rent Board Rules & Regulations § 4.12(a) states instead: 'A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable.' The codified Administrative Code § 37.3(a)(2) contains no 24-month condition at all.
Impact. MATERIAL to Unit 2. Under the 12-month rule (Rules § 4.12), the 1.6% available on 6/1/2026 banks on 6/1/2027 and the full 13.1% is recoverable. Under a literal 24-month rule it would not. Both units' scenarios in this analysis are computed on the 12-month rule because that is the text of the regulation and of the codified ordinance, and because SF.gov's own worked examples apply it.
Action. CONFIRM WITH A RENT BOARD COUNSELOR (415-252-4600, free) before serving any notice on Unit 2. Ask specifically which period controls under Rules § 4.12 and whether the info page's 24-month sentence reflects an amendment. Do not rely on either page alone.

F-06
HIGH
 Retaliation timing screen cannot be completed
Cannot be screened without owner records
Finding. Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5(a) bars a rent increase within 180 days of specified good-faith tenant complaints about tenantability, agency complaints, inspections or citations, or the filing of a proceeding. Section 1942.5(d) bars an increase made to retaliate for lawful tenant organising. Neither tenant's complaint, repair-request, inspection or organising history was supplied.
Impact. A mathematically perfect increase served inside a 180-day window is still unlawful retaliation. This is the single most likely way a correctly calculated increase fails review by a tenant lawyer.
Action. Before serving ANY notice, review each unit's records for the preceding 12 months: repair requests, communications about habitability, Department of Building Inspection complaints or citations, Rent Board petitions filed by the tenant, and any tenant-association activity. Document the review. If anything falls within 180 days, obtain legal advice before proceeding.

 Did Unit 1's 4/1/2026 rental agreement reset the anniversary date?
No official source resolves this fact pattern
Finding. Unit 1's lease commenced 2/1/2025. Two of three original occupants departed and one occupant was added, and a continuing tenancy is recorded at 4/1/2026 with rent unchanged at $3,995. SF.gov states the first annual increase may be imposed '12 months after the date the tenant's lease began', and that the anniversary date changes when an increase is imposed. No official SF source states whether executing a NEW written rental agreement with a replacement occupant — with no increase and with an original occupant still in place — resets the anniversary date.
Impact. If the anniversary date is still February 1, Scenario U1-A (3.0%, effective on or after 2/1/2027) is correct. If a new tenancy began on 4/1/2026, the first increase would not be available until 4/1/2027 and would use the then-unpublished 2027-28 percentage. Also review the 4/1/2026 document: whether it is a new lease, a roommate-change addendum, or a lease amendment materially affects the answer. Note that Rent Board Rules § 6.14 requires a written notice to subsequent occupants to preserve an unlimited-increase right later; whether one was served on the added occupant should be checked, because failing to serve it now will matter when the last original occupant eventually leaves.

Assumption requiring owner records

F-22
HIGH
 Rent increase license status unknown
Compliance gate — cannot be verified externally
Finding. An annual or banked increase imposed while the owner is unlicensed is NULL AND VOID in its entirety under Rent Board Rules § 13.14, regardless of whether the percentage and the notice were correct. License status is visible only through the Rent Board Portal and depends on the owner's own Housing Inventory filings. It cannot be verified from outside.
Impact. This is a complete defence for a tenant and the most commonly missed requirement. A 2-unit building has been within scope since March 1, 2023, so filings for March 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 must all have been made.
Action. Log in at portal.sfrb.org and confirm a current rent increase license is on file for BOTH units, and that the Housing Inventory submission for the current year was made by March 1. If any year was missed, file now — late submissions are accepted with no monetary penalty, but the license must be in effect ON THE EFFECTIVE DATE of the increase, not merely before the notice is served.
