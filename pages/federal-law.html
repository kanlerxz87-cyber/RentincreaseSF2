Federal Law

The Fair Housing Act and HUD. There is no federal rent ceiling for unsubsidised private housing.

Scope Federal law reaches these rent increases in exactly one way: anti-discrimination. There is no federal statute capping rent increases for unsubsidised private housing. HUD’s Fair Market Rents are a Section 8 program instrument and are not a ceiling on private market rents. Where federal subsidy is involved, HUD rent-reasonableness review applies — not relevant on the facts supplied.

Fair Housing Act protected classes
Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. §§ 3601-3619; § 3604
 The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing because of: Race, Color, National Origin, Religion, Sex, Familial Status, Disability. It applies to 'nearly all housing, including private housing, public housing, and housing that receives federal funding'. official source

FHA enforcement posture (2025-26)
HUD FHEO guidance, 'Fair Housing Act Enforcement and Prioritization of Resources'
 HUD's current published guidance states FHEO will prioritise 'cases with actual, provable instances of discrimination' and 'cases with strong evidence of intentional discrimination', and has withdrawn prior guidance documents. Note for the owner: this narrows FEDERAL administrative enforcement. It does NOT change California FEHA enforcement by the California Civil Rights Department, nor SF local law, nor a tenant's private right of action. official source

Federal law sets no rent ceiling
No applicable federal rent-control statute
 There is no federal statute capping rent increases for unsubsidised private housing. Federal law reaches rent increases only through (a) anti-discrimination rules, and (b) program rules where federal subsidy is involved (e.g. HUD rent-reasonableness review for assisted units). The operative caps here are San Francisco's Rent Ordinance and, residually, California AB 1482.
Stated as the absence of a federal cap; confirmed by the fact that both SF.gov and leginfo identify local and state law as the operative limits.
