California Law

Civil Code sections 827 (notice), 1947.12 (AB 1482 cap), 1942.5 (retaliation) and Government Code 12955 (FEHA housing discrimination).

The counter-intuitive point California’s 8.8% AB 1482 cap does not protect these tenants and does not limit this owner. Because San Francisco’s 1.6% annual limit is lower, Civ. Code § 1947.12(d)(3) steps the state statute aside entirely. A 13.1% banked increase is lawful here notwithstanding 8.8%. California’s real contribution is the notice mechanics (§ 827), the retaliation bar (§ 1942.5) and FEHA (Gov. Code § 12955).

AB 1482 statewide cap — 5% + CPI or 10%, whichever is lower
Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12(a)
 'An owner of residential real property shall not, over the course of any 12-month period, increase the gross rental rate for a dwelling or a unit more than 5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower, of the lowest gross rental rate charged for that dwelling or unit at any time during the 12 months prior to the effective date of the increase.' For SF that is currently 8.8% (5% + 3.8% CPI) for 8/1/2026-7/31/2027. No more than two increments in any 12-month period. official source

AB 1482 does NOT apply to these units
Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12(d)(3); SF.gov
 § 1947.12(d)(3) exempts 'Housing subject to rent or price control through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power consistent with Chapter 2.7 (commencing with Section 1954.50) that restricts annual increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a).' The SF Rent Board states plainly: 'This does NOT apply for units under the rent increase limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance.' SF's 1.6% is less than 8.8%, so the local ordinance governs.
BOTH UNITS — the 1.6% SF cap governs, not the 8.8% state cap

Retaliation presumption
Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5
 Civ. Code § 1942.5(a): where a lessor retaliates because of the tenant's exercise of rights or complaint to an agency about tenantability, and the tenant is not in default as to rent, the lessor 'may not recover possession of a dwelling in any action or proceeding, cause the lessee to quit involuntarily, INCREASE THE RENT, or decrease any services within 180 days' of the tenant's good-faith notice, oral complaint, written agency complaint, inspection/citation, or filing of a proceeding on tenantability. The 180-day period runs from the latest applicable date. § 1942.5(d) separately makes it unlawful to increase rent to retaliate because a tenant 'has lawfully organized or participated in a lessees' association or an organization advocating lessees' rights or has lawfully and peaceably exercised any rights under the law'. §§ 1942.5(c) and (e): to report or threaten to report the tenant, or individuals associated with the tenant, to immigration authorities is itself prohibited retaliatory conduct. § 1942.5(f): any waiver by the tenant is void as contrary to public policy.
BOTH UNITS — a timing screen must be run against each tenant's complaint/repair/organising history before any notice is served. Flagged item F-06.

Tenant harassment
SF Admin. Code § 37.10B; Cal. Civ. Code § 1940.2
 SF makes it unlawful for a landlord to use force, threats, menace or fraud to interfere with a tenant's quiet enjoyment, or to take actions intended to cause a tenant to vacate. Includes failing to perform repairs, removing housing services, abusing the right of entry, threatening the tenant, or influencing the tenant to vacate through misrepresentation. Tenants may sue for actual damages, or up to $5,000 per violation, plus attorney's fees; the City Attorney may also bring an action.

Just cause eviction (context)
SF Admin. Code § 37.9; Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2
 A landlord must have 'just cause' to terminate a covered tenancy. Mere expiration of a lease is not just cause. Relevant here only as context: a rent increase is NOT a substitute for eviction, and refusing to pay a LAWFUL increase is a basis for a non-payment action whereas refusing an UNLAWFUL increase is not. official source

Fair housing — California
Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 (FEHA), enforced by the California Civil Rights Department
 FEHA prohibits discrimination and harassment by housing providers based on protected characteristics, and applies to 'all aspects of housing and housing-related services, such as renting, leasing, sales, new construction, mortgage lending, insurance, municipal services, shelters, advertising, and restrictive covenants'. Retaliation for exercising rights is itself unlawful. California protects against discrimination based on the protected characteristic of a family member, friend, ROOMMATE, or any other associate.
