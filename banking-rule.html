Banking Interval (F-16)

Banking Interval — Open Question F-16

Official text compared F-16 remains open As of 2026-11-13 Not legal advice

What this page adds

This page compares the current official wording and worked examples for the banked-rent-increase interval. It does not declare the difference resolved. A prior audit did so; on rechecking, that conclusion overstated what the first worked example establishes and did not adequately qualify the second example’s timing language. F-16 is therefore open again.

  1. States both official formulations and the limits of what the published examples show.
  2. Keeps the calculations conditional. The site’s arithmetic uses the specific 12-month wording in Rules § 4.12(a) as a working assumption; that is not a legal determination that reconciles the other SF.gov summary.
  3. Refreshes date calculations from 2026-11-13. Dates are arithmetic on the notice rules, not a recommendation to serve.

No property-specific rent history, license, coverage fact or notice has been independently verified. See F-16, Suggestions & Decision Paths, and the historical audit at AUDIT.md.

1. The official wording differs

The Rent Board’s administrative rule and its SF.gov summary are both official sources, but they do not express the waiting period in the same terms. The site cannot silently treat one sentence as an authoritative gloss of the other.

Official sourceWording relevant to timingWhat remains unclearOfficial source link
Rent Board Rules § 4.12(a)A full 12 months must elapse from the date an annual increase, or a portion of it, could have been imposed before the banking section applies. The subsection also says a banked amount may be given only at the time of an annual increase.The rule gives a 12-month clock tied to the date an allowance could first have been imposed. How the summary’s 24-month sentence is intended to map onto that clock is not stated there.SF.gov — Rent Board Rules Part IV, § 4.12(a)
SF.gov — Banked rent increases“A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost.”The sentence does not identify, in the quoted wording, whether the 24-month clock is global from move-in/last increase or a separate clock for each skipped allowance. Its example must be read with its own dates and result.SF.gov — Banked rent increases
Rent Ordinance § 37.3(a)(2)Allows an owner who refrains from imposing an annual increase or a portion to accumulate it and impose it on a subsequent rent-increase anniversary date, subject to § 37.15.The ordinance states the banking right but does not set a 12- or 24-month interval in this subsection; it does not itself reconcile the two explanatory formulations.SF.gov — Rent Ordinance § 37.3

2. What the official worked examples do — and do not — establish

The examples are evidence about the Board’s intended calculations, but they do not amount to a published explanation reconciling the different 12- and 24-month wording.

Conclusion for this reviewThe rule gives a 12-month-from-availability formula; the summary says 24 full months without an increase; the examples include a result that appears to favor the rule’s formula but do not explain the summary wording.F-16 remains unresolved Do not publish “resolved,” “only interpretation,” or a legal conclusion based solely on the arithmetic comparison.Rent Board Rules § 4.12(a); SF.gov summary; SF.gov worked example

Open question. The public material reviewed here does not state in one place how the 24-month summary should be applied allowance by allowance, nor does it expressly explain why the worked example’s date span is written as shown. Until the Rent Board clarifies the intended rule or qualified legal advice resolves the interpretation, the site keeps F-16 open.

Provisional calculation method, not a conclusion of law: tables elsewhere apply the explicit § 4.12(a) 12-month-from-availability wording and label owner-specific totals conditional on that method and on the complete rent ledger. Do not rely on those calculations as legal clearance. Ask the Rent Board to reconcile the exact text and example; its counselors provide information but cannot give legal advice. A separate attorney review may be appropriate, but no free review or pre-approval is guaranteed. SF.gov — Rent Board counselor; SF.gov — Tenant and Landlord Resources

Separate timing wording — F-28 remains open. Rules § 4.12(a) also states: “This banked amount may only be given at the time of an annual increase.” Current guidance says a properly banked amount does not expire and may be imposed at another time when rent may be raised. The 2027-02-15 scenario pairs the banked 11.5% with the current annual 1.6%; it does not assume a standalone banked increase. That pairing is why the exact regulatory wording must not be suppressed, but it is not a legal clearance for these facts. Confirm the off-anniversary scenario with a counselor or counsel. The conservative alternative is 2027-06-01, whose new annual percentage and dollar total remain blank. Rules § 4.12(a); sf.gov — Banked increases; sf.gov — Irregular periods; F-28

7. Two further primary-source points worth recording

PointVerified textWhy it matters hereOfficial source
The license test is applied to the effective date, not the service date"Any rent increase under Ordinance Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2) purporting to be effective while the landlord is unlicensed shall render the entire rent increase null and void; provided, however, that the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used to determine whether or not the increase conforms."Confirms the sequencing advice on the main site and pins the test to a date the owner controls. A license obtained between service and the effective date is sufficient.sfrb.org — Rules §13.14; sf.gov — Form 538 (2026–27)
A void increase does not destroy banking rights"Nothing in this Regulation shall affect a landlord's banking rights or the ability to accumulate otherwise lawful rent increases that were found to be null and void."Materially reduces the downside of the F-03 license gap: a notice that fails for want of a license loses that increase, not the accumulated 11.5%. It is still a failure, and the tenant may file a summary petition, but the banked balance survives.sfrb.org — Rules §13.14; sf.gov — Tenant summary petitions
The license arises from substantial compliance with reporting"A landlord who has substantially complied with their obligation to report information about a rental unit as described above shall receive a license to increase rent for that unit under Ordinance Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2)." (Adopted July 12, 2022)Answers the individual-owner question at the level of the regulation: the trigger is reporting the unit, not the owner's corporate form.sfrb.org — Rules §13.13/§13.14; sf.gov — Sec. 37.3(a)(1)
Non-conforming increases are void under the ordinance itselfRent Ordinance §37.3(b)(5): "Nonconforming Rent Increases. Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void."The void consequence comes from the ordinance as well as from Rules §4.10(b) and §13.14, so it is not a technicality that can be argued away.sf.gov — Sec. 37.3(b)(5)
Discrepancy recorded (A-06): the license requirement's start dates differ between official documentsRent Ordinance §37.15(c), the Housing Inventory page, and the rent-increase page give 1 July 2022 for buildings of 10+ units and 1 March 2023 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. The Rent Board's live Oct 2026 Form 538 worksheet (2026–27) states the requirement applies to increases effective on or after 1 July 2021 (10+ units) and 1 March 2022 (condominiums and fewer than 10 units), updating its earlier 2024–25 PDF (which printed 2020/2021 and now returns HTTP 500).Low practical impact — enacted Ordinance §37.15(c) controls, all stated dates are long past, and a current license is required now either way — but it is an inconsistency between official Rent Board documents and is logged as irregularity A-06 in AUDIT.md and F-24. For a two-unit building the relevant group is "fewer than 10 units" on every version.sf.gov — Sec. 37.15; sf.gov — Housing Inventory; sf.gov — Form 538 (2026–27)
