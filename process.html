Correct way to inform tenants

StepRequirementSource

4. TimingOnly once every 12 months; effective date becomes the new anniversary datesf.gov

Reasonable reasons for the increase

The annual CPI-based allowable increase is the standard, legally recognized reason and is considered normal business practice for rent-controlled units in San Francisco — no additional justification is needed within the cap. sf.gov — Rent increases Increases above the cap require a Rent Board petition (capital improvements, increased operating/maintenance costs). sf.gov — Capital improvements

One unit or both? (discrimination avoidance)

Raise both units under the identical policy. Applying the same allowable percentage, same notice, and same effective-date rules to both units is the safest approach: differential treatment of tenants in rent-increase decisions can be challenged as discrimination in the terms and conditions of rental under the federal Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. §3604) and California law. SF tenants can obtain free tenant-advocacy and legal services and can petition the Rent Board (including unlawful-rent-increase petitions at no filing charge), so any increase should be documented as uniformly applied and within the cap.
