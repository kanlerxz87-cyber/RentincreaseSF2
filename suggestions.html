C. One unit or both units — equal-treatment options

The owner asked whether to raise one unit only or both, to avoid discrimination claims. San Francisco’s increase limits are computed unit by unit (each unit has its own base rent, ledger and anniversary), while fair-housing law prohibits decisions based on protected characteristics. The options below are for review; neither is a legal clearance. sf.gov — § 37.3

OptionWhat it involvesRisks to reviewStatusOfficial source
EQ-A — Same method and comparable timing for both unitsApply the same decision rule to both units (for example, the conservative annual-only option in each unit’s own lawful amount, or full available allowance on each unit’s own anniversary), documented in one written policy.Uniform treatment is easy to document and explain; the amounts will still differ because the base rents and ledgers differ. Retaliation (F-18) and protected-class screens still apply.Proposed — for owner reviewCRD — Housing; 42 U.S.C. ch. 45 (Fair Housing)
EQ-B — Unit-specific timing or amountsTake different options in the two units (for example, U2-C now and U1-A in February), each anchored to that unit’s own ledger, anniversary and notice arithmetic.Lawful in principle — the Ordinance is unit-specific — but the business reasons (ledger, anniversaries, pending legislation) should be in writing before service, and the pattern should be screened for disparate impact and for any appearance of retaliation or preference tied to protected characteristics.Proposed — for owner reviewCRD — Housing; Civ. Code § 1942.5 (retaliation)

Free or low-cost review is available to tenants; the owner should assume any notice will be read by a tenant lawyer and write it to survive that review: correct arithmetic, correct notice period, license current, no protected-class or retaliatory element, and consistent documentation across both units. No source can guarantee a tenant lawyer will approve an increase. sf.gov — Legal resources for housing discrimination
