San Francisco Law

The Rent Ordinance, Rent Board Rules and Regulations, and official SF.gov guidance. This is the operative ceiling for both units.

Why local law controls The SF Rent Ordinance is expressly intended to be more protective than the state baseline, and where it offers greater protection it applies rather than AB 1482. Both units are continuing tenancies in a two-unit building stated to have been built in 1940, so the local rules below are the ones that decide the answer.

Annual allowable increase
SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a)(1)
 60% of the increase in the CPI for All Urban Consumers in the Bay Area, published by the Rent Board each March 1, and never more than 7%. Currently 1.6% for increases effective 3/1/2026 through 2/28/2027. official source

One increase every 12 months
SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a); Rent Board Rules § 4.12
 'Under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance, a landlord can only increase a tenant's rent by a certain percentage once every 12 months.' The first annual increase may be imposed 12 months after the date the tenant's lease began. official source

Anniversary date
SF.gov Rent Board guidance
 'Once a landlord has increased a tenant's rent, the date of the increase becomes known as the tenant's anniversary date. The landlord cannot increase the rent again until at least 12 months later. If a landlord increases the rent more than 12 months later, the effective date of the increase becomes the tenant's new anniversary date.' official source

Banking is permitted
SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a)(2); Rent Board Rules § 4.12
 An owner who does not impose an allowed increase may bank it and use it later. 'Once an increase is properly banked, it does not expire.' Banked percentages are ADDED, not compounded. There is no limit on how much can be banked, back to April 1, 1982. New owners may use prior owners' banked increases. Banked increases do NOT carry over to a new tenant. official source

Tenants may replace roommates
Rent Board Rules §§ 6.15A, 6.15B, 6.15D, 6.15E
 The Rent Ordinance generally allows tenants to replace departing roommates and/or increase the number of occupants, even where a written lease prohibits it. Landlord has 14 days to approve or deny in writing with specific facts; silence or unreasonable denial is deemed approval. Occupancy caps: 2 in a studio, 3 in one-bedroom, 4 in two-bedroom, 6 in three-bedroom, 8 in four-bedroom, or the Housing Code § 503 maximum. official source

Algorithmic / revenue-management pricing is BANNED
SF Admin. Code § 37.10C
 Effective October 14, 2024, it is unlawful to sell, license or provide, or to use, an 'algorithmic device' that analyses non-public competitor data to recommend what rent to charge or whether to leave a unit vacant. Each separate month is a separate violation; each separate unit is a separate violation. City Attorney or a tenant/tenants' rights organisation may sue for damages, injunctive relief, restitution, civil penalties up to $1,000 per violation, and attorney's fees. ENFORCEMENT EXPANDED: as of October 6, 2025, tenants' rights organizations may ALSO file civil lawsuits to enforce the prohibition, in addition to the City Attorney and individual tenants (File No. 240796).
Answers 'how do big real estate corporations do this' — in SF they legally may not use pricing software.

NEW: demolition and major-renovation tenant protections, effective February 9, 2026
SF Planning Code and SF Admin. Code Ch. 37 as amended, effective 2026-02-09
 Adopted by the Board of Supervisors and announced January 23, 2026; amends BOTH the Planning Code and the Rent Ordinance. Adds a new just cause for eviction at Rent Ordinance § 37.9(a)(17) (permitted redevelopment requiring demolition of an entire building) and narrows § 37.9(a)(10) to permanent removal of a SINGLE unit. Requires replacement of all demolished units, sometimes as affordable or rent-controlled. Bars demolition permits for five years after certain tenant harassment or after a non-compliant buyout. Requires the Rent Board to hold an investigative harassment hearing before an ALJ within 45 days where criteria are met (no damages or monetary relief; appealable to the Rent Board Commission). For temporary capital-improvement evictions under § 37.9(a)(11), a lower-income tenant displaced more than three months is entitled to additional monthly payments for up to 39 months. Creates a rebuttable presumption that a tenant who moves out within one year of an owner/relative move-in notice moved out because of it, even if the notice was later withdrawn. Adds buyout disclosure requirements and an Ellis Act disclosure of any plan to demolish within five years.
Not engaged by a rent increase. Materially relevant if the owner ever considers capital-improvement work, a buyout, or withdrawal — see Flag F-24.
Also relevant to Flag F-10: this is now the authoritative official description of how the Rent Board handles tenant harassment reports.
