Door 1 — a market-rate reset is NOT available

The Rent Board's Topic 153 states that Rules and Regulations § 6.14 provides an unlimited rent increase on remaining occupants "when the last original occupant vacates the unit", and only if the landlord timely served every remaining occupant with a written "6.14 notice." On the supplied facts an original occupant (Tenant A) still lives in the unit, so the trigger has not occurred.

Door 2 — charging more because an occupant was added is unlawful

The Rent Board states plainly: the Rent Ordinance prohibits charging more rent solely because a tenant added an occupant to an existing tenancy, including a newborn child. This is an unlawful rent increase even if the lease or rental agreement specifically permits it. A tenant who has paid it may petition to void the increase and recover the overcharges.

Time-sensitive: the 6.14 notice window may already have passed

A § 6.14 notice preserves a future right: it tells a subsequent occupant that the landlord may raise rent without limitation once the last original tenant vacates. The Rent Board says it must be served within a reasonable time after the landlord knows or should have known of the occupancy, and that "generally, sixty days is considered reasonable."

What the household change does NOT change

  • The tenancy continues. Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant; a continuing tenancy with an original occupant is not a new tenancy. sf.gov — Banked increases
  • The license still applies. A current rent increase license must be on file before an annual or banked increase takes effect. The license is not required for a 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins increase or for passthroughs — but neither of those is available here. sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance
  • The anniversary date is still February 1 on the supplied facts, because no increase has ever been imposed. sf.gov — Irregular periods
