1. First question: as an individual owner, do I still need the Rent Board license?

Yes. There is no small-owner or individual-owner exemption. The Rent Board states that all owners of residential property must report into the Housing Inventory, and that owners who report a unit as tenant-occupied receive a Rent Increase License for that unit. The requirement turns on the unit being rent-controlled and tenant-occupied — not on whether the owner is a corporation.

Your building is in scope, and has been since March 1, 2023. The licensing rule applies to increases effective on or after July 1, 2022 generally, but for condominium units and buildings with fewer than ten residential units it applies to increases effective on or after March 1, 2023. A two-unit building falls in that second group.

It is not a separate licence application. Reporting into the Housing Inventory through the Rent Board Portal automatically generates the license for non-owner-occupied units. Reporting is free, online, and available 24/7. The annual deadline is March 1. There is no monetary penalty for filing late — but a late filing does not rescue an increase that already took effect unlicensed.

sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · Rent Board Rules § 13.14 · Rent Board Portal

2. Must I inform the Rent Board before notifying the tenants?

The formal legal deadline is the effective date of the increase, not the date you serve the notice. Rules § 13.14 says so in terms: "the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used to determine whether or not the increase conforms with the provisions of this section."

There is no separate per-increase filing with the Rent Board for an ordinary annual or banked increase. The official process is (a) annual Housing Inventory reporting, which produces the license, and (b) written notice to the tenant. The license does not have to be attached to the tenant notice. Above-allowance increases are different — those require a petition.

Recommended sequence anyway: complete the Inventory and confirm the license in the Portal before serving. It costs nothing, and it removes the single defect that voids an otherwise perfect increase.

Rules § 13.14 · sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
